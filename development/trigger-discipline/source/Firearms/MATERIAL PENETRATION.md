# MATERIAL PENETRATION

**At a glance:** External physical material can STOP, REDUCE, or be PENETRATED by a projectile; this is distinct from target PENETRATION through RES.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - Whenever PROJECTILE routing intersects external material before a target, resolve the material first using the projectile/effect's MATERIAL PENETRATION capability against the material's resistance/thickness profile.
> - **STOP:** the projectile ends in the material.
> - **REDUCE:** the projectile continues but with reduced remaining DAMAGE, target PENETRATION, MATERIAL PENETRATION, or other offensive performance according to the projectile/material profiles.
> - **PENETRATE:** the projectile continues with comparatively little loss, though destructible material can still be damaged by the passage.
> - MATERIAL PENETRATION is not the same value as target PENETRATION. Target PENETRATION is used after the effect reaches a character/object with RES; MATERIAL PENETRATION determines whether external walls, barriers, COVER, and similar material are defeated first.
> - Generic HIT QUALITY improvements to effective target PENETRATION do not improve MATERIAL PENETRATION unless an explicit ability, ammunition type, weapon property, or effect says so.
> - Repeated fire can degrade destructible COVER. A later projectile may therefore encounter different geometry from an earlier projectile in the same firing action.
> - Whatever remains after penetration continues to the next physical intersection in order.
> - CONCEALMENT does not provide ballistic protection merely because it makes a target harder to locate.
> - Exact material resistance, thickness categories, residual DAMAGE/penetration loss, and cover-damage conversion are balance/reference parameters.

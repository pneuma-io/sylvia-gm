# RESUSCITATOR

**At a glance:** CORPSMAN specialty that handles DOWNED casualties, catastrophic physiological failure, resuscitation, and preventing critical deterioration.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - RESUSCITATOR does not create a universal death timer and does not gate STABILIZE or RESUSCITATION attempts by other characters when fiction, skill, and equipment allow.
> - DOWNED and LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION are separate rules objects. A casualty can be DOWNED without actively dying; DEATH'S DOOR means at least one LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION is ACTIVE or FAILED.
> - The actual injury determines what is killing the casualty and whether a reversible path to survival exists.
> - Exact AP, deterioration, resuscitation, stabilization, and equipment values remain balance parameters.
>
> **ENTRY — RESUSCITATION:** If you begin a valid RESUSCITATION before the condition's reversible window expires, that window does not expire merely because the intervention requires several AP to complete. Continuous appropriate care must be maintained. Interruption, impossible physiology, equipment loss, further catastrophic injury, or another real end to reversibility can still close the window.
>
> **TIER 1 — Stop the immediate death**
> - **AIRWAY FIRST:** When airway or breathing compromise is part of an ACTIVE LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION, emergency treatment to restore/maintain breathing is faster and more effective than baseline CORPSMAN care. The actual obstruction/injury still determines the required intervention.
> - **MASSIVE BLEEDING:** Emergency treatment against catastrophic hemorrhage gains improved effectiveness beyond baseline CORPSMAN care. If the bleeding source cannot physically be controlled with available treatment, the ability cannot override that fact.
> - **RAPID ASSESSMENT:** When reaching a DOWNED casualty on DEATH'S DOOR, identify the most immediately lethal detectable LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION faster, reducing time before the first appropriate intervention.
>
> **TIER 2 — Hold the line**
> - **SUSTAIN LIFE:** After successfully STABILIZING a critical condition, the physical treatment maintaining that stabilization is more resistant to ordinary movement, delay, or battlefield handling. New injury, loss of the actual intervention, or failure of the treatment can still return the condition to ACTIVE where appropriate.
> - **CONTINUOUS CARE:** When a critical casualty requires ongoing manual treatment or monitoring, maintaining that care consumes less AP/attention. The medic remains physically occupied by the patient.
> - **RESUSCITATION KIT:** Specialized emergency equipment such as airway gear, defibrillation equipment, blood/fluids, monitoring, or setting-equivalent tools can be carried and brought into use with less handling/setup cost. Bulk, charges, consumables, and prerequisites remain.
>
> **TIER 3 — Bring them back**
> - **RETURN OF CIRCULATION:** When a casualty has a reversible FAILED LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION involving circulation or another equivalent failed vital function, successful RESUSCITATION produces a stronger controlled result after the normal FAILED → STABILIZED transition. Incompatible causes are unaffected.
> - **RESTORE CONSCIOUSNESS:** After successful RESUSCITATION, when the casualty's physiology and INJURIES genuinely permit consciousness, they regain consciousness sooner. They may still remain DOWNED at 0 HP and unable to take a normal full AP turn.
> - **CRASH INTERVENTION:** If a casualty currently being treated suddenly deteriorates because a known LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION worsens or returns to ACTIVE, the next appropriate emergency intervention costs less AP.
>
> **TIER 4 — Critical care**
> - **MOVE THE CRITICAL:** A STABILIZED DOWNED casualty being transported under the RESUSCITATOR's supervision is less likely to lose the physical intervention maintaining stabilization merely because of ordinary movement. Violent movement, new injury, loss of airway, lost hemorrhage control, or equivalent real failure still matters.
> - **BRIDGE TO TRAUMA:** After successfully RESUSCITATING/STABILIZING a casualty, the first TRAUMA or other definitive medical treatment performed on them gains improved effectiveness or reduced setup time because access, monitoring, and physiological control are already established. This can benefit another medic.
> - **REFUSE DETERIORATION:** While actively providing critical care to an ACTIVE LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION whose lethal cause cannot yet be fully controlled, delay or reduce that condition's ordinary deterioration progression. Conditions that physically progress too quickly, treatment that becomes impossible, or new catastrophic injury can still force failure.
>
> **TIER 5 KEYSTONE — NOT YET**
> - When a DOWNED casualty still has a physically reversible path to survival, the emergency sequence identify lethal problem → immediate intervention → stabilize → sustain → attempt RESUSCITATION where appropriate becomes dramatically faster and more reliable. Unsurvivable destruction, missing anatomy required for life, conditions beyond available medical technology, and casualties whose reversible window has genuinely passed remain outside the ability.

# ICON

**At a glance:** INFLUENCER specialty for reputation, recognition, public image, audience, social proof, and turning personal visibility into leverage.

> [!note]- Full rules
> ICON does not require worldwide celebrity at low level. Recognition may begin within one scene, profession, faction, community, subculture, locality, or other meaningful social sphere. Reputation, audience, public perception, status, or visible association matter only when they plausibly affect how people respond. Fame never overrides agency and does not guarantee access, obedience, or affection.
>
> Detailed progression below is **tertiary/current-working** for later all-class review. Exact reputation scale, audience reach, leverage values, timing, and similar numbers remain balance parameters.
>
> ## ENTRY — RECOGNITION
> Define the public reputation by which you are currently known. When someone actually recognizes you and that reputation is relevant to the interaction, the fact of your reputation immediately counts as usable LEVERAGE without needing to establish it first. The reputation does not have to be positive. Someone who has never heard of you gives no benefit.
>
> ## TIER 1 — Make Recognition Useful
> - **NAME OPENS DOORS:** When a gatekeeper recognizes you and your reputation is relevant to their organization or audience, requests for ordinary social access gain improved effectiveness. This does not bypass physical or legal security they have no authority to waive.
> - **READ THE AUDIENCE:** A successful SOCIAL READ against a gathered audience reveals more information about the shared mood, priorities, sympathies, or points of tension across the group rather than requiring you to read everyone individually. Individuals may still disagree with the crowd.
> - **PUBLIC INTRODUCTION:** When you openly introduce or endorse an ally to people who recognize you, that ally gains temporary LEVERAGE from your reputation during that social scene. They do not gain your reputation permanently.
>
> ## TIER 2 — Use Social Proof
> - **CROWD MOMENTUM:** After successfully influencing a visible audience, their reaction becomes additional LEVERAGE against another person who demonstrably cares about that audience's opinion. A person who genuinely does not care about the crowd is unaffected.
> - **MAKE AN ENTRANCE:** When your arrival is publicly recognized, your first relevant social action in that scene gains improved effectiveness. If you enter anonymously or nobody knows who you are, nothing happens.
> - **ENDORSEMENT:** When you publicly support a person, product, faction, cause, or claim, your existing reputation provides stronger LEVERAGE toward people who already respect or value that reputation. An audience hostile to you may react in the opposite direction.
>
> ## TIER 3 — Control the Public Moment
> - **BROADCAST:** Given access to a real communications channel—press, livestream, radio, social network, public-address system, or equivalent—you can deliver one social action to a much larger relevant audience without individually approaching each listener. Different audience groups can still react differently.
> - **MESSAGE DISCIPLINE:** When challenged publicly, hostile questioning or attempts to drag the discussion onto an unrelated damaging subject impose less difficulty on keeping your intended message as the focus. This does not make accusations disappear.
> - **REPUTATIONAL RESERVE:** When a public accusation or damaging SOCIAL FACT would create LEVERAGE against you, one well-established contradictory reputation fact can offset part of that leverage. Evidence can still overwhelm reputation.
>
> ## TIER 4 — Shape the Social Environment
> - **MAKE IT PUBLIC:** When an opponent has a clear reputational stake in how a dispute is perceived, moving the dispute into a real public or recorded setting causes that reputational stake to become additional LEVERAGE. You need an actual audience or recording channel.
> - **TRANSFER PRESTIGE:** PUBLIC INTRODUCTION may grant your temporary reputation LEVERAGE to multiple allies participating in the same visible social effort rather than only one. Their later conduct can still undermine the endorsement.
> - **DEFINE THE MOMENT:** After a highly visible event in which you personally participate, deliberately frame one true and publicly observable aspect of that event as part of your reputation. If the audience has evidence contradicting the framing, you do not overwrite reality.
>
> ## TIER 5 KEYSTONE — LIVING SYMBOL
> Within audiences where your reputation is genuinely established, RECOGNITION becomes a reusable social resource across the whole scene. Your reputation can simultaneously support your own social actions, allies you publicly endorse, a message you broadcast, and pressure against people whose status depends on that audience. You still cannot make people care about a reputation irrelevant to them, and a hostile audience may react against what you represent.

# ENFORCER

**At a glance:** BREACHER specialty that wins the physical contact-range fight by chaining CQC control and close firearm use.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - ENFORCER uses the current discrete CQC-action model: THROW, TRIP, SHOVE, DISARM, CONTROL, weapon retention, and close firing.
> - It does not create a grapple minigame or own entry-point creation.
> - Exact AP, CQC, DAMAGE, HIT QUALITY, and defense values remain balance parameters.
>
> **ENTRY — WEAPON RETENTION**
> - Gain improved CQC DEFENSE specifically against attempts to DISARM, CONTROL, redirect, or otherwise physically take a firearm being held out of the fight. Those maneuvers can still succeed normally if they beat the ENFORCER's retention.
>
> **TIER 1 — Fight around the weapon**
> - **WEAPON FRAME:** While holding a suitable long gun, using it as leverage improves a SHOVE or CONTROL attempt. If the weapon is lost, trapped, or unusable as leverage, the benefit disappears.
> - **MUZZLE CONTROL:** After successfully SHOVING or CONTROLLING an adjacent enemy, the next firearm attack against that same enemy gains improved attack effectiveness. The firearm must still have a valid firing line and usable state.
> - **RETENTION COUNTER:** When an enemy fails a DISARM or CONTROL attempt directed at the ENFORCER's held firearm, the ENFORCER's next CQC maneuver against that enemy costs less AP.
>
> **TIER 2 — Punish contact**
> - **PEEL OFF:** When an adjacent ally is being physically CONTROLLED, a CQC attempt specifically to break that control gains improved effectiveness. The attempt must still succeed.
> - **DISARM AND TAKE:** After successfully DISARMING an enemy, seizing the released weapon costs less AP if it is physically reachable and a hand is available. The weapon keeps its exact ammunition and chamber state.
> - **HEAVY CONTACT:** Successful SHOVE or THROW maneuvers also deal a limited amount of RES damage. The maneuver does not become a high-PEN melee attack.
>
> **TIER 3 — Chain the close fight**
> - **CHAIN MANEUVER:** After successfully resolving one CQC maneuver, the next different CQC maneuver against the same target costs less AP. Each maneuver still resolves independently.
> - **GUN-HAND TRANSITION:** After successfully resolving a CQC maneuver against an adjacent target, a firearm attack against that target costs less AP to bring into the sequence, and vice versa. The actual attack and maneuver still occur normally.
> - **FOLLOW THE FALL:** When THROW or TRIP puts an enemy PRONE, the next CONTROL or DISARM attempt against that target gains improved effectiveness. The follow-up maneuver must still be made.
>
> **TIER 4 — Own the immediate space**
> - **HOLD THE DOOR:** Gain a dedicated PREP ACTION against an enemy attempting to physically pass through the ENFORCER's immediate contact space. When triggered, make a normal SHOVE or CONTROL attempt. This is not a universal opportunity-attack rule.
> - **COUNTER-GRAPPLE:** When an adjacent enemy fails any CQC maneuver against the ENFORCER, the next CQC maneuver against that enemy costs less AP.
> - **CONTROLLED SHOT:** When making a CALLED SHOT against an enemy currently CONTROLLED by the ENFORCER, reduce the additional DIFFICULTY imposed by the called target. A physically valid line to the selected target area is still required.
>
> **TIER 5 KEYSTONE — OWN THE CLINCH**
> - Every successful close action against the same adjacent opponent can create efficiency for the next different close action: CQC → different CQC, CQC → firearm, or firearm → CQC. Each action still costs AP, resolves independently, can fail, obeys firearm state and geometry, and requires the target to remain at contact range. Failure or successful separation ends the chain.

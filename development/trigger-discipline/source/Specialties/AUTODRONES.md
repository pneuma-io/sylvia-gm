# AUTODRONES

**At a glance:** TECH specialty that deploys autonomous systems, gives them useful standing orders, and coordinates several machines without continuous manual piloting.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - Ordinary drones can follow basic autonomous instructions; AUTODRONES makes standing orders broader, cheaper to manage, and more tactically useful.
> - Drones remain limited machines rather than extra player characters.
> - Exact command, AP, sensor, attack, and unit-count values remain balance parameters.
>
> **ENTRY — TACTICAL AUTONOMY**
> - When assigning a STANDING ORDER to an autonomous drone, define a more complex trigger and response than ordinary operation permits, such as observe-and-report, follow-and-maintain-distance, patrol, or engage targets meeting a declared identification condition. The drone cannot improvise outside the order or exceed its sensors, mobility, weapons, or identification capability.
>
> **TIER 1 — Better orders**
> - **CONDITIONAL ORDER:** A STANDING ORDER may contain an additional simple condition: if X happens, do Y; otherwise continue Z. Conditions must be things the drone can actually sense or determine.
> - **AUTONOMOUS REPOSITION:** A drone executing a valid STANDING ORDER requires less operator AP/command overhead when relocating between positions required by that order. The drone still uses its own physical movement.
> - **HOLD POSITION:** A stationary autonomous drone may establish a prepared observation or weapon response appropriate to its equipment without continuous manual control. The trigger must be part of its STANDING ORDER.
>
> **TIER 2 — Multiple machines**
> - **SECOND ELEMENT:** Maintain useful STANDING ORDERS on additional autonomous drones without the normal increase in command/management cost. Each drone remains a separate physical unit.
> - **HANDOFF ORDER:** Changing one autonomous drone from an existing STANDING ORDER to another prepared order costs less AP. Communication with the drone is still required.
> - **SECTOR NETWORK:** Multiple drones assigned to observe adjacent or overlapping areas can share detections with the operator and one another, allowing one drone's observation to update relevant standing orders without manual relay. Signal connectivity is required.
>
> **TIER 3 — Useful independence**
> - **ESCORT ROUTINE:** An autonomous drone assigned to a moving ally can reposition itself to maintain a declared relative role such as forward observer, rear watch, overhead sensor, or weapon support without new movement commands every time the ally moves. It must physically keep up and navigate the terrain.
> - **REACQUIRE:** If a target being tracked briefly breaks LOS, the drone may search a limited nearby area under its existing order rather than immediately abandoning the task. It does not know where the target went.
> - **LOCAL RESPONSE:** An autonomous drone may respond to one additional predefined battlefield condition without waiting for a command, such as retreating to COVER when fired upon or shifting observation to a triggered sector. The response must be configured in advance.
>
> **TIER 4 — Drone team**
> - **COORDINATED COVERAGE:** Autonomous drones may divide a larger area among themselves as one coordinated observation task, reducing redundant overlap and increasing useful coverage. Walls and sensor limits still create real blind spots.
> - **DISTRIBUTED FIRE:** If multiple weapon-capable autonomous drones have valid firing solutions against the same target, their coordinated attack gains improved effectiveness compared with unrelated independent fire. Each drone still expends ammunition and uses its own physical line.
> - **AUTONOMOUS RECOVERY:** When a current order becomes impossible because of blocked route, lost target, depleted ammunition, or similar foreseeable failure, the drone may automatically fall back to a predefined secondary order. Without one, baseline stop/safety behavior applies.
>
> **TIER 5 KEYSTONE — MACHINE SQUAD**
> - Maintain a coordinated group of autonomous drones under a shared operational plan. Each drone has its own STANDING ORDER, one or more prepared contingencies, and can exchange relevant sensor information with the group. Changing the group's overall task is more AP-efficient than individually commanding every unit. They remain constrained by predefined logic, finite sensors/ammunition, physical routes, communications, and their actual autonomy.

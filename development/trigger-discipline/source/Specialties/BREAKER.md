# BREAKER

**At a glance:** BREACHER specialty that changes the entry problem by creating and exploiting new physical openings through defended barriers.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - BREAKER is not a breaching-shotgun specialty. Any appropriate physical breaching method may qualify.
> - Other characters may breach barriers normally; BREAKER gains better tactical-time access, effectiveness, and follow-through.
> - Exact AP, damage, penetration, tool, and resource values remain balance parameters.
>
> **ENTRY — TACTICAL BREACHING**
> - Using an appropriate tool, weapon, charge, or physical method specifically to defeat a barrier or access point costs less AP/time than for a normal character. The method must still be physically capable of defeating the material.
>
> **TIER 1 — Understand and attack the barrier**
> - **READ THE BARRIER:** After a brief inspection, automatically identify the most vulnerable observable breach points and whether currently available methods are likely capable of defeating them. Hidden traps, concealed reinforcement, and inaccessible internal construction remain unknown.
> - **BREACH READY:** One dedicated breaching tool that would normally be inconveniently STOWED may be configured as QUICK ACCESS through specialized carriage. Bulk and burden remain unchanged.
> - **FOCUSED BREACH:** When deliberately attacking a specific structural component such as a lock, hinge, frame, weak panel, or mounting point, gain improved breach effectiveness/PENETRATION against that component. Material still matters.
>
> **TIER 2 — Create a useful opening**
> - **BREACH AND FLOW:** After successfully creating a usable opening, the BREAKER's first movement through that opening costs less AP. OVERWATCH, hazards, difficult terrain, and enemy fire still work normally.
> - **ALTERNATE METHOD:** If a breach attempt fails or proves insufficient, changing to a different accessible breaching method and beginning the new attempt costs less AP. The second method must actually be available.
> - **CONTROLLED BREACH:** When the method can physically be controlled, choose to emphasize either a larger usable opening or reduced collateral damage. The method must support the result and the choice does not grant both benefits.
>
> **TIER 3 — Shape the battlefield**
> - **FIRING PORT:** Instead of a passage, perform a controlled breach that creates a deliberately positioned firing aperture with less time/setup than ordinary careful demolition. Remaining barrier geometry and material still determine COVER.
> - **RAPID SECOND BREACH:** After successfully breaching a structure, the next breach against the same continuous barrier requires less setup.
> - **CLEAR PASSAGE:** If the breach creates minor rubble, broken framing, glass, or similar movement-impeding debris, clear one usable passage as part of completing the breach. Major collapse still needs actual clearing work.
>
> **TIER 4 — Assault breaching**
> - **HARD TARGET:** Appropriate breaching methods gain improved effectiveness against high-resistance barriers. This never makes an incapable tool capable.
> - **WIDE BREACH:** A successful breach can extend into an adjacent section of the same destructible barrier, creating a wider opening at increased resource/tool cost without repeating the full setup process.
> - **WORK UNDER FIRE:** Reduce the additional penalty/risk imposed specifically by SUPPRESSION while performing a breaching action. Actual hits, forced movement, incapacitation, lost tools, or destruction of the breach point can still stop the work.
>
> **TIER 5 KEYSTONE — NO FIXED ENTRY**
> - Once the BREAKER has assessed a barrier and has a method physically capable of defeating it, the full sequence assess → prepare → breach → exploit takes dramatically less tactical time. A successful breach may be shaped as a passage or firing aperture where the method supports it, and can immediately feed BREACH AND FLOW. Impossible materials remain impossible.

# FLANKER

**At a glance:** GHOST specialty that turns asymmetric battlefield position into offensive advantage during active combat.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - FLANKER does not grant permission to flank. It improves movement, attack conversion, and team exploitation when geometry genuinely removes the protection of the target's current COVER.
> - Exact attack, damage, movement, and PREP values remain balance parameters.
>
> **ENTRY — ANGLE ADVANTAGE**
> - When attacking from a direction where the target's current COVER does not protect them, gain improved attack effectiveness beyond simply removing the cover benefit. A valid firing line is still required.
>
> **TIER 1 — Create the angle**
> - **FLANKING MOVE:** Movement toward a position that will cause a selected enemy's current COVER to stop protecting them costs less AP. The destination must actually create the qualifying angle.
> - **SHOOT AND SCOOT:** After resolving an attack with ANGLE ADVANTAGE, the first movement toward different COVER or a meaningfully different firing angle costs less AP.
> - **SIDE PRESSURE:** Establishing SUPPRESSION from an angle where the defenders' current COVER does not protect them requires less ammunition/time. Full COVER from that direction still blocks the firing solution.
>
> **TIER 2 — Make them solve two directions**
> - **CROSSFIRE:** After making an ANGLE ADVANTAGE attack, one ally with a substantially different valid firing line against the same target gains improved effectiveness on their next attack against that target during the current TEAM TURN. Both firing lines must actually exist.
> - **CUT THE EXIT:** After reaching a qualifying flank position, establishing OVERWATCH over a physically valid retreat route costs less AP. It watches only the declared zone.
> - **REORIENT PUNISH:** If a target moves into new COVER that blocks the current firing line, the FLANKER's next movement to establish another qualifying ANGLE ADVANTAGE against that target costs less AP.
>
> **TIER 3 — Exploit the exposure**
> - **EXPOSED TARGET:** A successful attack with ANGLE ADVANTAGE deals increased DAMAGE. RES, armor, and PENETRATION still apply normally.
> - **WEAK SIDE:** When making a CALLED SHOT from ANGLE ADVANTAGE, reduce the additional DIFFICULTY imposed by the called target. The selected location must be physically visible.
> - **FLANKING OVERWATCH:** After using FLANKING MOVE to establish a qualifying angle, establishing OVERWATCH from that position costs less AP. Movement and preparation still both occur.
>
> **TIER 4 — Stay ahead of the defense**
> - **DOUBLE ENVELOPMENT:** When CROSSFIRE is active, both qualifying firing lines gain an improved offensive benefit rather than only the ally receiving the setup benefit. The effect ends when a firing line breaks or the TEAM TURN ends.
> - **CHAINED FLANK:** When an ANGLE ADVANTAGE attack DOWNS or otherwise removes a target, movement toward a qualifying flank on another enemy costs less AP. The second enemy must present a real new angle.
> - **SHADOW THE COVER:** Gain a PREP ACTION: designate a visible enemy and prepare limited relocation. If that enemy moves specifically into new geometry that blocks the current line, spend the prepared movement to shift toward a new firing angle. The movement allowance and terrain remain physical limits.
>
> **TIER 5 KEYSTONE — ENCIRCLEMENT**
> - When the FLANKER and at least one ally maintain substantially separated valid firing lines against the same target, both shooters gain the offensive ANGLE ADVANTAGE benefit from the crossfire while the geometry supports it. The target can defeat this by relocating, breaking a firing line, using geometry that protects against both, suppressing/removing a shooter, or otherwise solving the real crossfire.

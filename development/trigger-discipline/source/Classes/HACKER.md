# HACKER

**At a glance:** Offensive systems intrusion: gaining access, understanding unfamiliar architecture, stealing privilege, manipulating connected systems, and making devices do things their owners did not intend.

> [!note]- Full rules
> HACKER turns plausible access into useful control under pressure. The class does not grant magic wireless access; the fiction must provide a real route such as a NODE, LINK, credential, local interface, exposed service, or other plausible access path.
>
> **Class baseline — TACTICAL INTRUSION**
> - At Level 1, once a valid access route exists, HACKER performs intrusion actions using LOCAL HACKING or NETWORKING with less AP/time than an ordinary competent character.
> - Exact efficiency is a balance parameter. This never creates an access route, privilege, LINK, NODE capability, or remote function that does not already exist in the fiction.
>
> **Shared HACKER baseline**
> - **GET IN:** identify and exploit a plausible access route.
> - **GO DEEPER:** once access exists, improve it by gaining privilege, extracting useful information, exposing additional capability, or creating a foothold where plausible.
> - **STAY QUIET:** manage DETECTION and MONITORING while intruding rather than assuming successful access is automatically unnoticed.
> - **DO IT FAST:** HACKER progression makes useful intrusion possible under tactical time pressure. Exact action economy and timing remain open.
>
> **Specialty directions**
> - The two locked major specialty directions are LOCAL HACKER and NETWORKER.
> - LOCAL HACKER goes deep on one directly accessible NODE: bypass, extract, modify, override, and implant.
> - NETWORKER moves through connected topology: discover, pivot, authenticate, escalate, operate, exfiltrate, and preserve footholds.
>
> **Hard boundaries**
> - HACKER is about subverting systems rather than deploying or operating physical technology for its intended purpose.
> - A compromised system cannot provide functionality its physical hardware/software does not actually possess. Hacking a camera may expose or alter its real functions; hacking a purely mechanical padlock does nothing.
> - Remote access cannot invent local-only functions, and local access does not automatically grant control of the wider network.
> - Security is represented through architecture and state such as ACCESS, PRIVILEGE, DETECTION, MONITORING, and FOOTHOLDS rather than a generic CYBER HP resource.
> - HACKER can learn ENGINEERING or DRONE OPERATIONS like any other character, but those skills do not turn HACKER progression into TECH progression.

# LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION

**At a glance:** An injury-driven lethal process with its own cause, deterioration trigger, stabilization requirement, and failure consequence; there is no universal death clock.

> [!note]- Full rules
> A LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION exists only when the casualty has an actual physiological problem capable of causing death. Each condition defines:
> - **CAUSE:** the physical mechanism threatening life.
> - **DETERIORATION TRIGGER:** the elapsed time, exertion, lost treatment, movement, new harm, or other event that makes this specific condition worsen.
> - **STABILIZATION REQUIREMENT:** the real intervention capable of stopping or controlling that lethal mechanism.
> - **FAILURE CONSEQUENCE:** what occurs if the condition progresses beyond control.
>
> Each condition is in one of three states:
> - **ACTIVE:** the lethal mechanism is operating and can worsen when its defined trigger occurs.
> - **STABILIZED:** the immediate lethal mechanism is controlled; the underlying INJURY remains and new harm or treatment failure can destabilize it where appropriate.
> - **FAILED:** the threatened vital function has failed. A reversible FAILED condition may still support [[RESUSCITATION]]; an irreversible outcome is death.
>
> Common reference families may include massive hemorrhage, airway/breathing failure, or circulatory arrest, but different injuries need not share identical timing or interventions. Exact deterioration intervals, DIFFICULTIES, equipment requirements, and medical values are balance/reference-data parameters.

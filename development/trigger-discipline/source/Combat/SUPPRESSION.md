# SUPPRESSION

**At a glance:** Spend AP and real ammunition to pour active fire onto and around a physical position or connected area. A character may remain protected behind blocking geometry, but exposing into that continuing fire can cause them to be hit.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - Any suitable firearm can establish SUPPRESSION; the mechanic is not class-exclusive. Automatic weapons are usually more efficient because they can commit useful volume more easily.
> - SUPPRESSION is **active fire**, not a fear effect, not a character condition, and not an OVERWATCH-style promise to begin firing later. The shooter is already firing into the declared position or area for the duration of the action.
> - To establish SUPPRESSION, spend AP and commit ammunition to a connected physical area or sector that the firearm can actually threaten from the suppressor's firing position.
> - The zone is limited by RANGE, LINE OF SIGHT, weapon orientation, available ammunition, and solid geometry. Larger or longer-lived zones require greater commitment. Exact AP, ammunition, zone size, and duration values are balance parameters.
> - Ammunition committed to SUPPRESSION is spent when the action is established. Those rounds represent the continuing fire that makes the zone dangerous even if nobody later exposes themselves.
> - The committed fire provides a finite suppressive exposure capacity. Exact conversion between ammunition commitment and available exposure resolutions is a balance parameter.
> - Baseline SUPPRESSION persists through the opposing TEAM TURN and ends at the start of the suppressor's next TEAM TURN unless broken earlier.
> - SUPPRESSION ends early if the suppressor leaves the firing position, loses the required firing geometry, stops controlling the weapon, reloads or uses the weapon for an incompatible action, becomes unable to fire, or voluntarily ends the action.
> - Being attacked or being inside another SUPPRESSION zone does not by itself cancel SUPPRESSION. Taking protection can end it if doing so physically removes the firing line required to maintain the zone.
>
> **Exposure into active fire**
> - SUPPRESSION does not magically harm a character who remains fully protected with no valid firing path from the suppressor.
> - The tactical pressure comes from the physical fact that the suppressor is already firing onto and around the protected position. A defender who peeks, crosses, rises, or otherwise exposes themselves is entering active fire.
> - A valid exposure event occurs when a character physically exposes themselves into the threatened firing solution. Examples include crossing open ground, stepping out from COVER, changing STANCE so the body becomes exposed, exposing to ATTACK or AIM, maintaining or establishing exposed OVERWATCH, or performing another task that physically requires entering the threatened line.
> - Actions performed safely behind blocking geometry do not create exposure merely because they are tactically important.
> - When SUPPRESSION is first established, a character already exposed inside the zone immediately creates an exposure event.
> - Continuous movement through multiple suppressed GRID spaces is one exposure event, not one event per square. Resolve at the first qualifying point of exposure.
> - The same character may create another exposure event later by performing a distinct new exposed action while SUPPRESSION remains active and exposure capacity remains.
>
> **Exposure resolution**
> - On a valid exposure event, pause the triggering movement or action at the first actual point of exposure.
> - If suppressive exposure capacity remains, automatically resolve one SUPPRESSION ATTACK against that exposure. This roll represents whether the already-active fire catches the character during the exposure; it is not a new decision by the suppressor to start shooting.
> - A SUPPRESSION ATTACK is a normal firearm attack using the ordinary physical pipeline: ATTACK ROLL → HIT QUALITY → PROJECTILE routing → DAMAGE / target PENETRATION → RES / HP. External material encountered during routing uses MATERIAL PENETRATION first.
> - Actual COVER, RANGE, RECOIL, ammunition state, armor, and projectile geometry apply normally.
> - SUPPRESSION ATTACKS use a suppression-specific firing modifier because the fire is being maintained across an area rather than built from a deliberate individual firing solution. AIM does not apply to the SUPPRESSION ATTACK. Exact modifier is a balance parameter.
> - If the exposed character remains capable afterward, the declared movement or action continues.
> - NERVE never changes whether a character is allowed to expose themselves and does not make suppressive bullets less physically dangerous. A character may choose to take the risk regardless of temperament.
> - There is no generic SUPPRESSED state to recover from. Abilities that interact with SUPPRESSION must change the active fire, exposure, geometry, ammunition, attack, or another real rule quantity rather than referring to recovery from an undefined condition.
>
> **RECOIL, COVER, and physical fire**
> - SUPPRESSION is real firing. Its ammunition commitment generates RECOIL according to the weapon and volume of fire.
> - Maintaining SUPPRESSION does not count as the weapon resting for ordinary RECOIL recovery.
> - When the declared zone is a defended position, committed fire is physically striking at and around that position. Blocking material can therefore receive impacts even if nobody exposes themselves; SUPPRESSION never makes those rounds disappear from the fiction.
> - If suppressive rounds intersect physical COVER or another material, they use the normal PROJECTILE, COVER, DESTRUCTIBLE COVER, and MATERIAL PENETRATION rules. The attack/cover routing procedure determines how committed fire and failed exposure attacks translate into specific cover impacts.
> - Separate overlapping SUPPRESSION zones are separate physical threats. A character exposing inside more than one valid zone can resolve exposure against each one if each suppressor has remaining capacity.
> - Friendly characters are not immune to the firing area. FRIENDLY FIRE rules apply when allies cross a suppressor's actual firing solution.
> - SUPPRESSION naturally contests OVERWATCH because an exposed watcher who remains on the firing line is exposing into active fire; choosing protection may preserve safety but can break the LINE OF SIGHT required for OVERWATCH.
>
> **SUPPRESSION vs OVERWATCH**
> - SUPPRESSION means: **I am already firing through this position; expose yourself and the active fire may catch you.**
> - OVERWATCH means: **I am watching this area and will begin a prepared shot when the declared trigger occurs.**
> - Both use physical geometry, but they model different battlefield behavior and must not be collapsed into the same action.

## Balance parameters
- setup AP
- ammunition commitment
- zone size / depth
- persistence duration
- suppressive exposure capacity
- SUPPRESSION ATTACK modifier
- RECOIL generated by committed fire

# CQC ACTION

**At a glance:** Spend AP on one declared close-quarters maneuver. Roll once against derived CQC DEFENSE; success produces that maneuver's physical result and nothing else automatically.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - CQC requires actual physical reach, normally adjacent GRID positioning unless anatomy, weapon reach, barriers, elevation, or other geometry says otherwise.
> - Resolve a CQC maneuver with `1d20 + CQC + relevant APTITUDE + situational modifiers vs CQC DEFENSE`.
> - The defender does not make an opposed roll.
> - One CQC ACTION attempts one declared result. A failed maneuver spends its AP and produces no free counterattack, automatic DAMAGE, reversal, or hidden secondary effect.
> - Multiple maneuvers may be chained only by spending AP on multiple CQC ACTIONS. A character may SHOVE and then DISARM, for example, but those are two actions and two checks.
> - Adjacency does not create a universal ENGAGED state. Leaving physical contact does not create a universal opportunity attack unless CONTROL, a PREP ACTION, or another explicit rule says otherwise.
> - Firearms do not become unusable merely because an enemy is adjacent. A firearm may be used at contact range if it can physically be brought to bear and remains operable; weapon HANDLING may modify the attack. A successful WEAPON CONTROL can prevent the controlled weapon or limb from being brought to bear.
> - Default APTITUDE tendencies for benchmark play: FORCE commonly applies to SHOVE, THROW, and BODY CONTROL; PRECISION commonly applies to TRIP, DISARM, and WEAPON CONTROL. The normal relevant-APTITUDE rule still governs when circumstances clearly suggest otherwise.
> - Exact AP costs, contact-range firearm modifiers, maneuver difficulties, and forced-movement distances are balance parameters.

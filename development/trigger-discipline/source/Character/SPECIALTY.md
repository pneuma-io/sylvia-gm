# SPECIALTY

**At a glance:** Level-by-level tiered progression that begins with a fixed SPECIALTY ENTRY ability, then deepens or redirects a CLASS through four choice tiers and a final KEYSTONE.

> [!note]- Full rules
> - A character takes their first SPECIALTY progression choice at Level 1 and another meaningful specialty choice at every later level.
> - Specialty names describe progression directions, not exclusive subclasses. A character may go deep in one direction or invest across several directions when prerequisites allow.
> - The first investment in a specialty direction costs one SPECIALTY progression choice and grants that specialty's fixed ENTRY ABILITY.
> - An ENTRY ABILITY establishes the foundational qualification, piece of specialized training, state, action, or interaction that makes the specialty function. It is not one of the three-choice tier abilities.
> - ENTRY ABILITIES must not invent permission for ordinary physics or other baseline rules to function. If any character standing behind physical COVER is already protected by that geometry, a specialty node cannot simply grant "real" or "better" cover without defining an actual rules change.
> - Specialists do not automatically own an underlying SKILL or equipment category. An ENTRY ABILITY may represent trained use of specialized equipment or a new specialty mechanic, but ordinary characters may still interact with the underlying equipment or situation when the core rules allow.
> - After gaining the ENTRY ABILITY, TIER 1 in that specialty becomes available.
> - TIERS 1–4 each contain three available abilities in a finalized tree.
> - Taking any two abilities from a tier unlocks the next tier in that same specialty direction.
> - TIER 5 contains a single KEYSTONE ability rather than another band of three choices. It is unlocked by satisfying the normal progression requirement in TIER 4 and costs a SPECIALTY progression choice to acquire.
> - A straight path from ENTRY through KEYSTONE therefore requires ten SPECIALTY progression choices: one ENTRY, two choices in each of TIERS 1–4, and the KEYSTONE.
> - A KEYSTONE should be a defining culmination of that specialty's playstyle: transformative and distinctive, but still grounded in the CLASS identity and the game's physical rules.
> - The tier structure is the primary internal choice structure. Do not add nested subtrees or additional branching inside every specialty merely to create more choices.
> - SPECIALTIES should primarily grant new actions, interactions, efficiencies, state changes, equipment synergies, mobility changes, positioning tools, or other class-defining capabilities rather than relying on flat percentage improvements.
>
> ### Ability validity gate
> Every proposed ENTRY, tier ability, and KEYSTONE must pass all of the following before it is treated as a valid node:
> - **BASELINE COUNTERFACTUAL:** Ask whether an ordinary competent character could already do exactly what the node describes under existing rules. If yes, the node is invalid unless it provides a material advantage beyond baseline.
> - **MECHANICAL DELTA:** The node must change at least one real game quantity, state, action, interaction, or tactical possibility. Valid deltas include AP/time, movement, risk, attack effectiveness, DAMAGE, defense, RES, PENETRATION, ammunition/resource use, range/area, geometry, equipment access/capacity, state creation or persistence, trigger timing, information, recovery, or another explicitly defined system value.
> - **NO PERMISSION PERKS:** A node cannot merely authorize movement, following an ally, using COVER, carrying ordinary equipment, taking an action, or receiving a physical benefit that baseline rules already allow. Existing legality is not a reward.
> - **COMPETENT-NONSPECIALIST COMPARISON:** The designer must be able to complete the sentence: "Compared with another competent character in the same situation, this specialist can..." with a concrete advantage. If the answer only restates baseline play, the node fails.
> - **SPECIALTY IDENTITY:** The advantage must deepen the actual specialty's problem-solving identity rather than serving as a generic useful combat perk placed there to fill space.
> - **NO INVENTED SUPPORTING SUBSYSTEM:** A specialty node cannot rely on an undefined state, resource, restriction, action taxonomy, or other rules object created solely to make the node work. Define and accept the underlying core mechanic first, or redesign the node using existing rules.
> - **DEPENDENCY AUDIT:** Higher-tier nodes cannot depend on a lower-tier mechanic that itself fails this validity gate. If a foundational node is removed or revised, dependent nodes must be re-evaluated rather than preserved automatically.
> - **UNRESOLVED IS BETTER THAN FILLER:** During design, a tier may be shown with fewer than three abilities and an explicit UNRESOLVED slot. Do not invent a permission perk, restated physics, vague flavor, or mechanically redundant option merely to make the draft structurally complete. The three-choice requirement applies to the finalized tree, not to an early proposal.
> - Every valid ability should still be expressible as: **when it applies → what actually changes → what limitation remains.** If that cannot be stated cleanly, revise or cut it.
>
> - AP efficiency is only one design lever, not the organizing principle of classes.
> - Exact numeric figures such as AP discounts, movement distance, DAMAGE, RES, RECOIL, ammunition counts, area sizes, and similar values are balance parameters unless explicitly locked. Conceptual specialty design may specify the direction of a mechanical delta without fixing its final number.
> - When an ability expressly offers a choice between alternative benefits, costs, or outcomes, choose one alternative when that ability is activated or configured. Alternatives from the same choice do not stack unless the ability explicitly says they do.
> - SKILL and APTITUDE advancement are separate progression layers from the guaranteed SPECIALTY choice at each level.

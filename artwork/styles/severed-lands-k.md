# Severed Lands K art guide

K is anime character illustration with heroic fantasy lighting. The owner's supplied V portrait
is the rendering benchmark. Faces, hair and silhouettes should read as designed anime drawing
before the viewer notices painted materials. This guide tightens the selected K direction;
the current catalogue's visual match remains under review.

## Rendering brief

Draw original heroic fantasy characters with a clearly anime foundation and a controlled painted
finish. Use the supplied style image to judge drawing, edge control and shading. Each subject's
own references determine identity, age, species, build, complexion, hair and costume.

Faces: construct a deliberate stylized face with clean cheek/jaw silhouettes and readable eye
shapes. Give the upper eyelid a confident dark edge; design the iris, pupil and small catchlight
as graphic shapes. Make brows and mouth economical expressive marks. Describe the nose through
a small designed contour and broad light/shadow shapes. Keep the face recognizable through
shape and expression, with restrained surface detail.

Age and anatomy: retain the actual subject's age, proportions and distinctive features. Older
anime faces use mature silhouettes and a few intentional age lines; children retain their own
proportions. Scars keep their established side and shape. Species and creatures retain their
authored anatomy. The reference character's youth, face and body are never a template for others.

Hair: design large interlocking locks and ribbon-like groups with a clear silhouette. Use
selective interior lines, curved highlights and a few flyaways to explain those groups. Preserve
the subject's own cut, colour and hair texture; braids, curls and cropped hair remain distinct.

Shading: begin with broad, intentional shadow shapes that keep the drawing legible. Use controlled
soft transitions within those shapes to give painted depth; keep key facial and hair edges crisp.
Warm ivory key light and cool coloured shadows support the drawing. Keep skin smooth at normal
viewing size, with age marks chosen deliberately. Surface grain, pores, dirt and scratches are
secondary local details rather than a finish covering the entire face or costume.

Colour: preserve distinct local colours and the subject's natural complexion. Pale linen reads
as pale linen; charcoal, plum, olive and auburn remain distinguishable under the same light.
Use coloured shadows and selective accents to keep dark fantasy readable. Restrict amber light
to illuminated planes instead of washing every material in a bronze filter. Never borrow the
style character's skin or hair colour to achieve the match.

Materials: explain cloth folds, leather seams and metal edges with selected shapes and highlights.
Place texture where it describes a particular material or authored wear. Ornament belongs to
the subject's established design. A cook's apron and a guard's practical uniform receive the same
careful illustration as a scholar's cape without gaining its embroidery, jewellery or silhouette.

Composition: give the subject a clear silhouette and character-appropriate expression. Keep
complete heads, hair and equipment inside usable margins. Portraits retain recognizable eyes,
face shape and expression at small UI sizes. Plain dark grounds suit reference studies; actual
environment and lighting follow the requested asset. The final image must retain the benchmark's
anime drawing even when its colour, age, species or clothing is very different.

## Reference workflow

1. Read the subject's authored canon and saved appearance. Mark concealed details, pronouns,
   species, age and equipment that must survive every view. Keep editable defaults separate from lore.
2. Supply the exact style benchmark as the final reference at **every stage**. Earlier generated
   images carry identity; they do not replace the original style benchmark.
3. Full body: subject identity references first when available, style last. Show the complete
   subject with inspection margins. Establish costume and gear topology here.
4. Multiview: accepted full body first, style last. Keep the same subject across front,
   three-quarter, side and back views. Preserve anatomical sides and concealment.
5. Portrait: accepted body and multiview first, style last. Keep the head horizontally centred,
   eye line around 40% of image height and full hair/head silhouette within comfortable margins.
   Save adjustable crop metadata for Map, Party and dialogue rather than altering the originals.
6. Editing: identify the edit target separately. Retain the subject's identity references and exact
   style benchmark; change only the selected rendering or design property.

Label image roles explicitly: **subject identity**, **edit target**, **rendering style**. The style
image's face, white forelock, cape, symbols and accessories belong to that pictured person.
They are not features to distribute across the cast.

For creatures, apply designed silhouettes, edges and selective painted shading to the actual
anatomy. A nonhuman head stays nonhuman; an unknowable or concealed face stays concealed.

For map underlays, put the rendered map-data reference first and the style image last. Keep the
exact bounds and orthographic top-down geometry. Carry the guide's colour separation and chosen
edge/detail treatment into terrain and architecture; the pictured person supplies no layout,
portrait lighting, compulsory night scene or mechanical information.

## Visual check before expanding a batch

Compare at the same display size against the exact benchmark and the subject's earlier reference.

- Do the face and hair still read as anime drawing, including in older or nonhuman subjects?
- Are eyes, nose and mouth designed shapes, with texture subordinate to the drawing?
- Do local colours remain distinct, and does the actual complexion survive the lighting?
- Is the subject recognizable without inheriting the benchmark character's features or outfit?
- Are identity, age, concealment and equipment consistent across every view?
- At portrait and token sizes, are the face/head and outer silhouette readable without clipping?

Record a failure as visual work still open even when hashes, dimensions and source bindings pass.
Correct one fixed-design example and compare it before spending on another batch. Do not count
an image as a style match merely because the prompt contains the guide or the file checks pass.

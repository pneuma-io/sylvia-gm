## Rendering brief

Draw original anime-influenced heroic fantasy with confident, designed contours and selective
painted depth. Construct faces, hair, bodies and silhouettes through drawing from the beginning.
The supplied style image governs line work, shadow design, material treatment and finish;
each subject's own selected references and authored facts govern identity.

Faces: design expressive eye silhouettes, strong tapered upper lids and readable iris/pupil
shapes. Use economical brows and mouth marks, a chosen nose contour and clear facial light planes.
Vary cheek/jaw structure and proportions by the person. Mature faces retain age through their
shape and selected forehead, eye and smile lines. Keep ethnicity, complexion, scars and species
specific to the subject. A convincing older person, sturdy worker or unusual creature need not
share the benchmark character's face or proportions.

Hair and organic surfaces: use grouped masses, purposeful strands and clear outer silhouettes.
Braids, curls, coarse hair, cropped cuts, fur and feathers have their own structures. Place
interior lines and highlights where they explain those forms. Preserve the selected person's
own hair; the pictured braid and pale forelock are character details, not cast-wide motifs.

Shading and materials: begin with broad intentional shadow shapes, then add controlled soft
transitions where they give useful volume. Keep facial construction and important contours clear.
Cloth, leather, fur, stone and metal receive selected texture, seams, wear and highlights appropriate
to the material. Texture supports the drawing rather than covering every surface with one grain.

Colour and light: preserve distinct local colours and the subject's natural complexion. Use
coloured shadows and deliberate accents to keep dark materials readable. Warm key light and
cooler shadows are useful, while the actual asset may call for different light. Keep pale cloth,
dark leather, coloured fabric and skin distinguishable instead of washing them all in amber.

Design: establish a new or explicitly reimagined first-party character from authored identity,
role, species, age facts, equipment and the accepted visual anchors. Editable descriptions derived
from superseded production art are not constraints. Once a design is selected, retain its
canonical references for subsequent views and ordinary edits. User-selected character art and
imported identity references retain their own established appearance.

Rift creatures: make psychedelic influence part of material, growth and perceived structure.
Recursive mineral cavities, nested organic growth and impossible internal depth are useful
directions. Choose a coherent idea appropriate to the species, with a readable primary silhouette
and a few major distorted forms. Keep actual head/limb counts, authored materials, concealment
and fixed geometry clear. The Rift Cube's outer shape remains a perfect translucent cube; its
impossible space belongs inside it. Violet mineral sheen belongs to the Ankheg's authored shell.
Organic predators retain convincing weight and living anatomy while their spatial/growth logic
can become unsettling. Colour supports these structures; saturation alone is not the effect.

Apply Rift distortion according to the authored creature and context, with room for both visibly
altered and extreme forms. The Verdant Margin supports lush disorienting growth; the Cairnfields
and Rift pools support stronger mineral/spatial disturbance. Human Riftborn retain their own
selected designs. Visual distortion adds no new gameplay ability.

Composition: show a clear subject, complete head/hair or creature extremities, and usable margins.
Follow the requested full-body, multiview, portrait or map format. The benchmark's waist-up pose
is not a universal crop. Preserve readable head/eye forms at portrait size and retain adjustable
focal point/zoom. For maps, carry drawing, colour and material treatment into the supplied layout;
the pictured person supplies neither terrain geometry nor compulsory night lighting.

## Reference workflow

1. Read authored identity and mark actual appearance facts, pronouns, species, concealed details
   and equipment. Preserve unresolved mysteries as unresolved. For replacement catalogue designs,
   use `python -m scripts.prepare_authored_design_inputs` to separate marked editable artwork
   appendices from original text. Its design-text hash is not a replacement for a binding hash.
2. Establish the design using those facts. Use only the exact accepted identity master for each
   of the four people; use the Marla benchmark as rendering guidance for other fresh designs.
   Earlier superseded artwork and its generated appearance notes stay outside production inputs.
3. Supply the exact rendering benchmark last at every image stage. Label input roles explicitly:
   subject identity, edit target, rendering style. The pictured person's facial geometry, braid,
   skin tone, apron, forelock and pose remain their own.
4. Full body: selected identity references first when available, style last. Show the complete
   subject, feet/base and extremities within inspection margins. Establish costume/gear topology
   and creature structure before deriving later views.
5. Multiview: selected full body first, style last. Preserve the same identity across front,
   three-quarter, side and back views, including anatomical sides, concealment and gear placement.
   In a front view, anatomical right appears image-left; in a rear view it appears image-right.
   A profile facing image-right shows the subject's anatomical right side; a profile facing
   image-left shows its anatomical left. Check asymmetric equipment against the body master.
   Captured production prompts are historical provenance; any reversed side wording is
   superseded by this convention, not a direction to repeat the error.
6. Portrait: selected body and multiview first, style last. Keep the head centred and eye line
   around 40% of height where anatomy permits; keep the full head/hair silhouette inside margins.
   Save one adjustable crop for Map, Party and dialogue instead of altering the original images.
   Retain the full square illustration and check a closer head crop at small avatar sizes.
   The starting cast uses approximately 1.5x zoom for visible faces and 1.4x with adjusted focal
   points for the hood and two-headed hound. Choose framing per subject, preserving concealment
   and every required head. Match the actual head size rather than forcing one zoom value;
   smaller faces within a square illustration may need a closer crop. Compare at 64 and 96
   pixels before accepting the avatar crop.
7. Edits: identify the target separately and retain selected canonical references. An explicit
   new-design experiment can start again from authored facts, then establish its new references.

Both full-body and multiview references are required for a complete bundle. A selected concept
image alone is not complete coverage. New references join production canon when selected against
the authored facts and this guide. Individual creature studies remain outside canon until selected.

boundaries where the map defines them. Tokens, grid, labels and hidden geometry belong to the
application's mechanical presentation, not generated underlay art.

## Saved user work and catalogue production

This canon reset governs first-party Severed Lands production. A player's chosen images,
Character File, imported card and campaign remain their own selected work. A changed default
does not regenerate existing art or retry an old request. Captured style records remain available
for saved user requests and provenance; those historical records are not approval to use
superseded images for new first-party catalogue production.

The four accepted anchors and the finished reference workflow must replace the historical
premade assets before canonical catalogue/trailer acceptance. Technical file counts do not
establish that replacement.

## Visual check before expanding production

Compare at the same display size with the accepted benchmark and selected subject reference.

- Do face, hair and body still read as designed anime-influenced drawing for this age/species?
- Are anatomy, complexion, concealment and equipment consistent across every selected view?
- Is the subject distinct without inheriting another character's face, hair, outfit or pose?
- Does Rift influence meaningfully affect material/structure while the actual creature stays clear?
- At portrait/token size, are the head and outer silhouette readable without clipping?

File hashes, dimensions and packaging checks establish integrity. Style, design and canon still
need visual judgement. Record an unsuccessful study as open visual work, correct the cause, then
compare again before extending its treatment to a batch.

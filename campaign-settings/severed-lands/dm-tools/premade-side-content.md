# Premade Side Content — Severed Lands

Plug-and-play combat encounters and side distractions. Grab one when the player wants to fight something or the session needs filler between major beats. Each block includes: location, tier, setup, combat, and resolution.

---

## The Stolen Cache
**Location:** Verdant Margin (Anchor Clearing) | **Tier:** Low

**Setup:** A Returner supply cache at a Margin Anchor Clearing has been raided — food, medicine, and a Warden fragment gone. Tracks lead deeper into the Margin. The Returners offer a finder's fee.

**Combat:** 2d4 Hollowlings have claimed the cache as their own. They're not hostile by default — they're hungry, scared, and willing to trade the Warden fragment for food. If the party attacks, they fight with desperate pack tactics and flee when half are downed. If the party negotiates, they learn the Hollowlings were driven from the deep Margin by "something worse." The something worse (1 Riftbear Mauler) arrives during the conversation.

**Resolution:** Return the fragment for payment and gratitude. The Hollowlings, if spared, become recurring Warrens contacts — they make their way to Thornwall and remember the party's mercy. Or: the Mauler's territory now has no Hollowlings to warn travelers, making future Margin patrols more dangerous.

## The Pack Leader
**Location:** Verdant Margin (deep) | **Tier:** Mid

**Setup:** Sister Candle has found the riftbeast pack that killed her community. She's asking for help. She's not asking for mercy. The pack is denning near a Rift pool — regeneration zone, tactical depth, high risk.

**Combat (3 waves):**
1. **Pack Scouts (2 Riftwolves):** Testing the party, hit-and-run. Will retreat toward the pool if wounded.
2. **Hunters (2 Riftwolf Hunters):** Phase-step flanking, targeting the weakest party member. Fight near the pool — Hunters regenerate if not pulled away.
3. **Pack Leader (1 Riftwolf Pack Leader):** Energy arcs between any remaining pack members. Can call reinforcements — 1d4 additional Riftwolves arrive in 1d4 rounds. Kill the Leader, and the pack breaks.

**Resolution:** Candle's ledger gets one entry closer to full. She thanks the party — the first real warmth in her voice since her community died. The pool remains — a resource for future encounters, a hazard for future travel. The pack's territory becomes available for scavenging (increased loot on future Margin rolls).

## The Pillar Ambush
**Location:** Cairnfields | **Tier:** Mid

**Setup:** A Rift Raider warband (Ashen Claw or Silent Wing) has been hitting caravans on the western route. The Thornwall Council is offering a bounty. The Returners want the Raiders' intelligence — they've been mapping Door sites in the Cairnfields.

**Combat (choice of approach):**
- **Direct assault:** 2d4 Rift Raiders on the ground, 1d3 on Pteranodon-back (Silent Wing only). Raiders use coordinated tactics, focus fire on spellcasters, retreat to secondary positions if pressed.
- **Stealth approach:** The Raiders' camp is on a pillar-top, accessible only by climbing routes they've memorized. Stealth checks to infiltrate, find the intelligence, exfiltrate. Combat only if discovered.
- **Negotiation:** Korr (Ashen Claw) or Vex (Silent Wing) will parley if the party demonstrates strength first — defeat a raiding party, and they'll talk. What they want: recognition as a sovereign force in the Cairnfields, or information about the missing Doors.

**Resolution:** Bounty collected, intelligence acquired. The intelligence reveals Raider knowledge of a Door site in the deep Cairnfields — a lead the Returners will pay handsomely for. Diplomatic resolution establishes a fragile truce with that warband (future encounters may be neutral rather than hostile).

## The Breathing Street
**Location:** The Hollows | **Tier:** Low-Mid (atmospheric)

**Setup:** The Breathing Street — a residential road in the Hollows that phases more frequently than anywhere else in the ruins — phased back this morning. The buildings are different. There's a light on in one of the windows. Scavengers are avoiding it.

**Investigation:** The party enters the street. The buildings are pre-Rift townhouses — furniture visible through windows, personal effects still in place, as if the residents stepped out minutes ago. The light is coming from a second-floor window. Inside: a Warden artifact, active and humming — a sphere that projects star charts no one recognizes. The artifact is tied to the street's phasing pattern. Removing it might stabilize the street. It might also destabilize something else.

**Combat (optional):** The street is home to 1d4 Rift Ghouls and 1 Rift Shadow — Echoes of the original residents, replaying fragments of their lives. They don't attack unless the artifact is touched. If combat occurs, the street begins to phase — CON saves each round or take Rift damage as reality flickers.

**Resolution:** Artifact recovered. The Athenaeum will pay a fortune. The Returners will offer a fortune in gratitude. Or the party keeps it — and discovers it shows routes to locations that don't exist yet. The street stabilizes (if artifact removed) or continues its accelerated phasing (if left in place).

## The Jackal King
**Location:** Eastern trade route | **Tier:** Mid

**Setup:** Gareth has called a moot — all Jackal bands, meeting at a neutral site on the eastern route. The purpose: unite the bands under a single leader. Gareth wants to be king. Half the bands support him. Half would rather burn the network down. The Thornwall Council is watching. The Guild is terrified.

**The Moot:** The party can attend as representatives of any faction (Returners, Thornwall, independent). The bands present: Red Banner (Gareth), Dusk Boys (Shrike, calculating), Tollmen (neutral, just want the bridge), Cairn Rats (desperate, easily swayed). Gareth makes his case. The opposition leader — a Tollmen representative or a wildcard from a smaller band — makes theirs. The party can influence the outcome.

**Combat (if negotiations fail):** The moot erupts. Gareth and his loyalists fight the opposition. The party picks a side or stays out of it. If Gareth wins: the Jackals become a unified force under a single commander — more organized, more dangerous, but also more predictable. If Gareth loses: the bands scatter, the routes become more chaotic, and Shrike emerges as the default alternative.

**Resolution:** The trade routes change. A unified Jackal network means safer travel for those who pay tribute, more dangerous travel for those who don't. A fractured network means unpredictable violence. Either way, the Guild adapts. The council reacts. The party's choice echoes.

## The Pool Cutter
**Location:** Thornwall Warrens | **Tier:** Low (investigation)

**Setup:** Madame Thistle needs a Rift pool found and destroyed. Someone in the Warrens has been cultivating one — a small pool, in a basement, feeding it Warden fragments. She's offering her territory's loyalty to whoever helps. Auntie Salt knows who the cultivator is. She's waiting for the right buyer.

**Investigation:** Track the cultivator through Warrens informants (Kit, Grim, Saffron's patients). The cultivator is a Riftborn whose child is dying of a Rift-touched illness — the pool is an attempt to expose the child to controlled Rift energy, hoping it will trigger a healing response. It's working. The child is improving. The pool is growing.

**Resolution paths:**
- Destroy the pool, arrest the cultivator (Thistle's gratitude), and find another way to help the child (Henna, Cassian, or Saffron may have alternatives).
- Let the pool remain, help the cultivator hide it (cultivator becomes a contact, the pool becomes a resource for Rift-touched healing experiments — but it keeps growing).
- Report it to the council (Thistle disavows you, the cultivator is executed, the pool is sealed, and the Warrens Court never trusts you again).

## The Warden Chamber
**Location:** The Hollows (northern sector) | **Tier:** Mid-High

**Setup:** Kell's Iron found a sealed Warden chamber — sigils active, door intact. They're auctioning expedition rights. Three factions are bidding: the Returners (want research access), the Athenaeum (want exclusive publication rights), House Kael (wants whatever's inside, no questions asked). The party is hired by one of the bidders.

**The Chamber:** Once opened, the chamber reveals a Warden training hall — preserved weapons, intact armor, inscriptions covering every wall. The inscriptions describe a Warden combat technique that the Lodge has never recovered. The weapons are Echo-bondable. The armor is centuries old and still humming.

**Combat:** The chamber's guardian — an Anchor Sentinel upgraded to an Anchor Guardian — activates when the central artifact is touched. The Guardian is a boss-tier construct: high AC, Warden-forged weapons, tactical combat protocols. It fights with the skill of a Warden Knight. It doesn't negotiate.

**Resolution:** Artifacts recovered, technique documented. The faction that hired the party gets first access. The Lodge will pay anything for the technique. The Athenaeum will publish. Kael will use whatever's inside for her own purposes. Choose who benefits, and make an enemy of the other two.

## The Elder Stirs
**Location:** Cairnfields (Deep Rift) | **Tier:** High (endgame foreshadowing)

**Setup:** Old Crane has been signaling for days. The Deep Rift is changing. The Elder Riftbeast beneath it — the oldest and largest creature the Rift has ever touched — is stirring. The Cairnfields are trembling. Riftbeasts are fleeing the region. Something is about to happen.

**The Scene:** The party arrives at the Deep Rift's edge. The pool is churning — violet-black energy surging in patterns that don't match any known Rift behavior. Tallow is there, or has been there — her equipment is scattered near the shore. She's gone. So is whatever she was trying to find.

**Combat (if the party enters):** The Deep Rift's energy manifests as Rift Elementals — 1d4 Rift Fire, 1d4 Rift Water, 1 Rift Weird at the center. Fighting them is not the point. Surviving them while witnessing what's happening beneath the surface IS. The Elder's shape is visible in the depths — massive, ancient, slowly waking. It's not hostile yet. It's not anything yet. It's just... becoming aware.

**Resolution:** The Elder doesn't awaken fully — not this time. The Rift calms. The tremors stop. But Old Crane's signal was right: the Elder is closer to waking than it's been in centuries. The endgame clock is now ticking. Tallow's equipment is recovered (she's missing). Her notes describe what she felt in the Veil before she went in — a voice, calling her name, from something that shouldn't know her name.

## Using These Blocks

- **Reskin for your party's level:** Add or remove enemies, adjust stats, change tier. The setup stays the same; the challenge scales.
- **Connect to the campaign:** Each block ties into existing factions and NPCs. The Breathing Street artifact is the same sphere mentioned in Lady Kael's research. The Warden Chamber's technique is the same one the Lodge has been searching for. Everything connects.
- **Use when:** The player says "I want to fight something," the campaign needs a breather between major story beats, or the party wanders into territory the DM hasn't prepped for.
- **The blocks are starting points, not scripts.** The DM fills in details based on party composition, recent events, and where the campaign is heading.

# The Anchor Church

**Type:** Religious order (Anchor Domain clerics)
**Headquarters:** A small chapel in Nexus, near the Athenaeum
**Territory:** Congregations in Nexus, Thornwall Warrens, Waystation
**Size:** ~50 ordained clergy, ~300 regular congregants

## Overview

The Anchor Church is the Severed Lands' youngest faith — and its fastest-growing. Its central belief is simple and radical: the Anchor Doors are not Warden technology. They are sacred. They were built not by a dead civilization but by something closer to divine — the Wardens as prophets, not architects. And the Doors' purpose was never just travel. It was refuge.

The Church was founded thirty years ago by a cleric named Sister Iron who experienced what she described as a revelation while touching a dormant Door fragment. The fragment, she claimed, spoke to her — not in words but in certainty. The Doors exist to provide a way home. Not just for Riftborn. For everyone. The Severed Lands is a waiting room, and the Doors are the exit.

This theology is controversial. The Athenaeum dismisses it as mysticism. The Returners are uncomfortable with it — they want the Door to work, not to be worshipped. But the Church's message resonates with Riftborn who've lost hope and with settled folk who've never had any. The Church is growing because it offers something no other faction does: a guarantee that home is real, that the Doors will open, and that there's a reason for all of this.

## Beliefs

The Anchor Church's doctrine is still being formalized, but the core tenets are:

- **The Doors are sacred.** They are not machines, not artifacts, not technology. They are holy. The Wardens were not engineers — they were the first congregation, the builders of the refuge.
- **The Rift is a trial.** Not a wound, not a transformation — a test. The Severed Lands is a crucible. Those who endure it are being prepared for something. What that something is, the Church hasn't yet revealed — because the Doors haven't opened, and the full truth can only be known on the other side.
- **Home exists.** The Church's most powerful promise: every world that Riftborn remember is real. Every home is still there, waiting. The Rift didn't destroy them. It separated them. And the Doors will reunite them.
- **Service is preparation.** The faithful are expected to aid Riftborn, protect Door sites, and resist those who would destroy or exploit the Doors. Good works aren't just moral — they're rehearsal. The way you live in the Severed Lands determines what you find on the other side.

## Leadership

- **Sister Iron:** Founder and spiritual leader. Iron is old now — her hands shake, her voice cracks, but her conviction is undimmed. She preaches every seventh day in the Nexus chapel, and the crowds spill into the street. Iron has never claimed to speak for the Doors. She says she only repeats what they told her. The fact that no one else has heard the Doors speak is, she says, because no one else has listened properly.
- **Father Glass:** The Church's diplomat. Glass handles the relationships the Church needs to survive — with the Athenaeum (cautious), the Returners (awkward), the Thornwall Council (hostile). He's patient, soft-spoken, and utterly unflappable. When Sister Iron says something theologically explosive, Glass is the one who explains it in terms the secular factions can tolerate.
- **Deacon Ash:** The Church's enforcer. Ash leads the Door Wardens — armed congregants who protect Door fragments and Church gatherings from those who'd disrupt them. Ash was a Thornwall guard before he found faith. He still moves like one. The council considers him a deserter. He considers himself liberated.

## Congregations

- **Nexus Chapel:** The Church's heart. A converted warehouse near the Athenaeum, filled with salvaged Door fragments arranged as an altar. Services are standing-room-only. The Athenaeum's scholars attend out of anthropological curiosity. Some of them keep coming back.
- **Thornwall Warrens Shrine:** A basement room in the Warrens, maintained by a single cleric named Mother Wick. The shrine is the only place in Thornwall where Riftborn can worship openly — the council considers the Church a "Riftborn religion" and doesn't interfere. Mother Wick's congregation is small but fiercely loyal. She's performed more Riftborn funerals than anyone in the city.
- **Waystation Chapel:** A recent addition — a small room near the Door chamber, maintained by a Returner who took Anchor Domain vows. The other Returners are uneasy about it. The Door is a research subject, not an altar. But the cleric argues that faith and research aren't mutually exclusive. The debate is ongoing.

## Tensions

The Church's growth is creating friction:

- **With the Athenaeum:** The Church claims the Wardens were prophets. The Athenaeum insists they were a civilization — advanced, yes, but mortal. The debate is academic for now. If the Church's influence continues to grow, it won't stay academic.
- **With the Returners:** The Church supports the Returners' mission but frames it in theological terms that make V uncomfortable. The Returners want to open the Door. The Church wants to reveal the Door. The difference sounds semantic. It isn't.
- **Internal:** A faction within the Church — the Revealers — believes the Church should be more militant. They want to seize control of Door sites, demand access to Warden artifacts, and force the issue. Sister Iron has refused. The Revealers haven't given up.

## Hooks

- Sister Iron has announced that the Doors will speak again — to someone, somewhere, within the year. She doesn't know who. She doesn't know where. She only knows what the fragment told her. Every faction is watching. Everyone wants to be the one the Doors choose.
- Father Glass has been meeting with Athenaeum scholars in secret — not for diplomacy, but for research. He's been comparing Church doctrine with Warden inscriptions, looking for evidence that supports the faith's claims. What he's found disturbs him. It's not that the evidence contradicts the faith. It's that the evidence is older than the Wardens.
- The Revealers are planning something. Mother Wick in the Warrens has been hearing whispers — a Door site in the Cairnfields, a plan to occupy it, a demand for Returner cooperation. If the Revealers move, the Church will fracture. Sister Iron knows. She's been praying about it.

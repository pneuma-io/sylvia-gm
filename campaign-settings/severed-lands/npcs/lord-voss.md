# Lord Aldric Voss

Lord Voss is the wealthiest and most powerful member of the Thornwall Council — and the most vocal opponent of Riftborn integration. He controls the farmland surrounding Thornwall, the grain supply, and the livestock trade. His family has held their council seat for seven generations. He believes, with genuine conviction, that he is protecting the Severed Lands from a threat.

Voss is not a villain in his own mind. He's a patriarch who's watched Rift surges coincide with Riftborn arrivals for his entire life. He's seen the Hollowed — people he knew, people he grew up with — transformed into something unrecognizable. He's buried friends who were killed by riftbeasts that didn't exist before the Rift started spreading. He's drawn a conclusion: Riftborn are connected to the Rift, the Rift is destroying the world, therefore Riftborn are dangerous. It's not malice. It's a lifetime of evidence pointing in one direction, and no one has ever shown him evidence pointing anywhere else.

His son died six years ago — killed by a Rift surge while traveling the eastern route. Voss doesn't talk about it. His politics did.

## Personality

Voss is formal, deliberate, and used to being obeyed. He doesn't shout. He doesn't need to. When Lord Voss speaks in council, the room goes quiet — not because he's intimidating, but because he's been right often enough that people listen. He treats opposition with patience rather than hostility — he believes his position is correct and that time will prove it. This makes him more dangerous than a rabid zealot. A zealot can be dismissed. Voss can't.

He genuinely believes Riftborn are victims as much as threats — souls displaced by the Rift, deserving of compassion, but too dangerous to integrate into settled society. He advocates for "assisted settlement" — designated Riftborn zones, supervised travel, regulated trade. He doesn't see this as oppression. He sees it as protection.

## Role in a Campaign

**As antagonist:** Voss is the face of institutional anti-Riftborn sentiment. He pushes for registration requirements, travel restrictions, and segregation policies. He's not the final boss — he's the obstacle that makes every Riftborn's life harder. A campaign that aligns with the Returners or the Stayers will clash with Voss repeatedly.

**As potential ally:** Voss is not unreachable. Evidence that Riftborn arrivals don't cause surges — or that something else is accelerating the Doors' decline — could shift his position. He's a man who follows evidence, even when that evidence contradicts his conclusions. A campaign that presents him with proof he can't dismiss might turn an enemy into an uncertain ally. He won't apologize. He will change his vote.

## Behind the Screen

Voss's anti-Riftborn policies are genuine, but they're also politically useful. Keeping Riftborn marginalized keeps the Warrens contained, keeps the Returners at Waystation, keeps Thornwall's settled population comfortable. Voss doesn't think of it this way — but his politics and his power are mutually reinforcing, and he's never had to examine that too closely. A campaign that forces him to confront the consequences of his policies — the lives destroyed, the families separated — might break him. Or it might make him double down.

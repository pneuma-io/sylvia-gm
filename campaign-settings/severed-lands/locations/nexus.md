# Nexus

**Type:** City (cultural and scholarly capital)
**Region:** Central plains, at the crossroads of major trade routes
**Population:** ~8,000 (mixed — settled, Riftborn, travelers from across the Severed Lands)

## Overview

Nexus is the largest city in the Severed Lands and the only major settlement that actively welcomes Riftborn. Built at the intersection of three major trade routes, it grew from a waystation for caravans into the cultural and scholarly heart of the region. If Thornwall is the Severed Lands' marketplace, Nexus is its soul — louder, messier, more diverse, and far more dangerous to the established order.

The city's defining feature is the Athenaeum — a sprawling complex of libraries, lecture halls, and research chambers dedicated to the study of the Rift, the Wardens, and the Anchor Doors. Scholars from across the Severed Lands come to Nexus to study. What they find there often changes them — not always for the better.

Nexus is governed by a loose coalition of guilds, academic factions, and neighborhood councils. It's chaotic, inefficient, and surprisingly functional. No single family controls Nexus the way the merchant houses control Thornwall. Power shifts constantly, alliances form and dissolve, and anyone with enough coin or knowledge can find a seat at the table.

## The Athenaeum

The Athenaeum is the Severed Lands' greatest repository of knowledge — and its greatest concentration of people who should not be trusted with what they know. The complex began as a single library built around a dormant Anchor Door fragment. Over generations, it expanded: new wings, deeper archives, laboratories for studying Rift phenomena, and the increasingly fortified chambers where the most dangerous Warden artifacts are kept.

The Athenaeum's scholars are organized into schools, each focused on a different discipline:
- **School of Warden Studies:** The largest and most prestigious. Dedicated to deciphering Warden sigils, mapping Door sites, and piecing together the Wardens' history. Their work directly informs the Returners' research, though the relationship between the two groups is complicated.
- **School of Rift Dynamics:** Studies the Rift itself — its surges, its pools, its effects on living things. This school has the highest casualty rate. Rift exposure is an occupational hazard, and their scholars tend to become Hollowed within a decade of joining.
- **School of Cartography:** Maps the Severed Lands — no small task when geography shifts with every Rift surge. Their maps are the most valuable intelligence asset in the region. Both the Returners and the Thornwall Council pay fortunes for updated editions.
- **School of Medicine:** The Athenaeum's physicians are the best in the Severed Lands. They treat Rift-touched conditions that no one else understands, study the Hollowed with more compassion than fear, and maintain the uncomfortable fact that most Rift-related ailments have no cure — only management.

The Athenaeum's relationship with the Returners is one of mutual need and mutual suspicion. The scholars have access to knowledge V needs. V has access to Door sites the scholars can't reach. They collaborate when their interests align. They hoard information from each other when they don't.

## The Districts

- **Scholar's Quarter:** The streets around the Athenaeum. Bookshops, scriptoriums, alchemical suppliers, and the boarding houses where visiting scholars live. The Quarter is quiet during the day (everyone's studying) and raucous at night (everyone's arguing about what they studied). The taverns here host debates that occasionally turn into brawls that occasionally turn into published papers.
- **The Crossroads:** The market district where the three trade routes meet. More chaotic than Thornwall's market, more diverse, and far less regulated. You can buy anything at the Crossroads — Warden fragments, Rift-touched curios, weapons from worlds that don't exist, information that will get you killed. The only rule is: buyer beware.
- **Riftborn Quarter:** The largest Riftborn community in the Severed Lands. A neighborhood built by and for people who fell through the Rift — its architecture a patchwork of styles from a dozen different worlds. The Quarter has its own governance, its own guard, and its own unofficial ambassador to the Nexus council. It's not a ghetto. It's a statement: Riftborn built this, and it's still standing.
- **The Undercroft:** Beneath Nexus, a network of pre-Rift tunnels and catacombs. The Athenaeum uses part of it for secure storage. The rest is claimed by smugglers, Hollowed refugees, and things that moved in before the city was built. The Undercroft connects to the Hollows — a fact the council officially denies and everyone unofficially knows.

## Atmosphere

Nexus feels like a city that's still being invented. Every wave of Riftborn arrivals brings new ideas, new architecture, new food, new languages. The streets are loud. The politics are messy. The Athenaeum's scholars are always on the verge of discovering something that will either save the Severed Lands or doom it — and no one can agree which discoveries fall into which category.

There's an energy in Nexus that doesn't exist anywhere else in the Severed Lands: hope. Not the desperate, fragile hope of the Returners waiting for a Door to open. Something steadier. The belief that the Severed Lands is not just a waiting room and the Rift is not just a wound — that something new is being built here, something that might outlast the Doors and the Wardens and everything that came before.

## Hooks

- An Athenaeum scholar has published a paper claiming to have translated the Wardens' symbol — the circle bisected by a line. The translation is a single word. The scholar refuses to say what it is without "appropriate security." Three days later, their laboratory is ransacked and the scholar is missing.
- The School of Rift Dynamics is recruiting subjects for an experiment involving controlled Rift exposure. They're offering payment generous enough to attract volunteers. The last batch of subjects stopped answering to their names.
- A Riftborn community leader in the Quarter has been receiving threats. The messages are written in Warden script — a language no living person can write. Someone in Nexus knows more about the Wardens than the Athenaeum does. They're not happy about the scholar's paper.

# Siren Protocol Campaign Setting Guide

## Status

Siren Protocol is an early-development future-Earth cyberpunk Campaign Setting for Trigger Discipline. The present year is **2225**.

The [Setting Constitution](SETTING_CONSTITUTION.md) defines setting-wide truths. [World Architecture](WORLD_ARCHITECTURE.md) defines the current geography, orbital, registry, reproductive-industry, Australian, and showcase-region architecture.

## Relationship to Trigger Discipline

`Trigger Discipline Ruleset > Siren Protocol Campaign Setting > individual Campaign Plans`

Trigger Discipline defines generic mechanics and may support present-day or other compatible eras. Siren Protocol selects a future expression of those mechanics.

See the [Ruleset Bridge](RULESET_BRIDGE.md) before writing material that touches mechanics.

## Setting promise

Siren Protocol takes place on Earth in 2225, roughly two centuries after the Severance reduced natural male births to approximately one for every hundred thousand female births.

Humanity survived and adapted. Biological pregnancy remains ordinary; archived male genetic material supports assisted conception at civilisation scale; every naturally born male is registered by a relevant jurisdiction and owes one genetic contribution intended to preserve male genetic diversity.

The setting is not a permanent demographic emergency. Female society has had generations to normalise new relationship, family, sexual, and institutional structures. Male-centred polyamory is common without becoming compulsory. Female/female relationships are ordinary. Jealousy, monogamy, exclusivity, individual orientation, and disinterest remain real.

Power is fragmented among governments, successor states, megacorporations, corporate city-states, gangs, syndicates, private wealth, local authorities, unions, advocacy bodies, and autonomous enclaves. Earth's geography remains recognisable, but two centuries of political change mean present-day borders are not assumed to survive intact.

Technology is recognisable future cyberpunk rather than magic: real firearms, cybernetics, neural interfaces, hacking, drones, AI, advanced medicine, barriers, vehicles, biotechnology, satellites, orbital stations, and limited lunar industry/research.

## Present Earth

The current year is **2225**.

Siren Protocol is overwhelmingly Earth-based. Satellites, orbital stations, military/scientific/commercial orbital platforms, and limited lunar facilities exist, but there is no default interplanetary civilisation or major off-world population comparable to Earth.

Campaign Plans may use orbital or lunar locations when useful without turning the setting into space opera.

## Politics and geography

Modern geography remains legible. Australia, Japan, Europe, North America, Africa, South America, and other major geographic/cultural regions still mean something.

Political continuity is intentionally looser. Current states may survive in evolved form, merge, fragment, federalise, corporatise, devolve power to cities, lose territory, or be replaced by successor states.

No world government exists. No single megacorporation secretly controls everything.

## Australia in 2225

Australia became a major strategic power through two tightly linked systems: **desert-scale solar energy** and enormous **data-centre / compute infrastructure**.

Its inland landscapes support solar fields, storage, transmission corridors, compute campuses, and service settlements. Coastal regions remain the largest concentrations of population, finance, ports, logistics, culture, and network exchange.

Power did not transfer through one corporate coup. It moved gradually away from the old Canberra-centred federal/state hierarchy as public government became increasingly dependent on private owners of energy, compute, communications, transport, logistics, medicine, reproductive infrastructure, housing, finance, and security.

The erosion was cumulative: long leases, privatisations, public-private concessions, outsourced services, privately built extensions, corporate financing, lobbying, corruption, revolving-door careers, and short-term political incentives repeatedly made the next concession easier than rebuilding public capacity. The result is a **concessionary oligarchy** rather than a clean post-democratic corporate state.

Government still exists and matters. Courts, elections, taxation, policing, regulation, welfare, diplomacy, public administration, and public services remain real.

But the practical system is now a **competitive corporate oligarchy layered over surviving public institutions**. Corporations are the dominant power centres, not one unified corporate state. They compete with one another, government bodies, unions, communities, syndicates, gangs, private security organisations, wealthy patrons, and other interest groups.

Canberra remains a major constitutional, administrative, judicial, archival, diplomatic, and inter-jurisdiction centre. It is no longer accurate to describe it as the single command point from which Australia is governed.

## Showcase region: Sydney coast

The setting's authored showcase is the **Sydney coastal megaregion**, growing from Greater Sydney through the Illawarra/Wollongong corridor, Central Coast, and Newcastle/Hunter region.

The final 2225 administrative boundaries and official regional name are not yet locked.

Sydney concentrates population, capital, culture, network exchange, corporate headquarters, advanced medicine, reproductive services, private security, logistics, finance, manufacturing, research, and high-value technical work while depending heavily on inland energy and compute infrastructure.

Its default political texture is:

**some government, primarily competing corporate oligarchies, plus smaller interest groups and gangs.**

Government can still legislate, arrest, tax, regulate, operate courts and services, and sometimes defeat corporate interests. Corporations remain dominant because they control too much essential infrastructure for government to treat them like ordinary private firms.

Sydney is a patchwork of both public and private jurisdiction. Public law can remain formally sovereign while corporations dominate practical life through infrastructure ownership; elsewhere companies possess explicit territorial or functional powers under old concessions, leases, charters, or development compacts.

No single corporation owns Sydney. Competition between corporate oligarchies creates space for public institutions, gangs, syndicates, unions, Aboriginal organisations and land interests, communities, investigators, operators, and player characters to matter.

### Jurisdiction stack

For street-level play, authority can exist simultaneously at seven levels:

1. constitutional sovereignty;
2. municipal/regional law;
3. land ownership;
4. infrastructure concession;
5. network/identity authority;
6. contracted security;
7. practical street control.

A public street can sit over a corporate station, inside a privately owned development, authenticate through LATTICE, use PALISADE security, and border a neighbourhood where a community organisation or gang has stronger local practical influence than any of them.

## The six principal Australian oligarchies

Australia's major corporate blocs are sprawling conglomerates with **primary industrial spines**, not neat sector monopolies.

### INLAND Systems Group

**INLAND** grew from desert solar, storage, transmission, hyperscale compute, and AI infrastructure. It supplies unusually cheap and reliable power and compute to public institutions, universities, medicine, research, and business, while its control over capacity, pricing, cooling water, and model access gives it enormous political leverage.

### Meridian Transit & Development

**MERIDIAN** grew from transport concessions, freight, stations, ports, and transport-led development. It now controls the principal high-speed trunk linking Sydney with Brisbane and Melbourne. Its network is fast, safe, and genuinely excellent; its control over mobility chokepoints, station property, ticketing identity, and concession boundaries is equally formidable.

### Morrow Genomics & Health

**MORROW** grew from hospitals, reproductive medicine, genetics, cybernetics, pharmaceuticals, and genetic-bank infrastructure. Its care is legitimately world-class and foundational to ordinary life after the Severance. That same position gives it structural power over medical continuity, reproductive access, intimate data, genetic provenance, and registry-adjacent systems.

### Lattice Civic Systems

**LATTICE** grew from networks, finance, payments, insurance, identity, credentials, and cross-jurisdiction trust systems. It makes fragmented Australia function smoothly and with low fraud. It can also make a person economically or administratively disappear when account, identity, insurance, or trust decisions cascade across other systems.

### Palisade Defence & Security

**PALISADE** grew from private security, defence systems, drones, intelligence, emergency response, and critical-infrastructure protection. Its rescue, disaster, anti-trafficking, and kidnapping-response capabilities are widely respected. It is also private coercive power with variable contractual policing authority and deep intelligence reach.

### Bluehaven Urban Systems

**BLUEHAVEN** grew from water, housing, property, utilities, municipal outsourcing, and coastal resilience. It keeps enormous parts of urban Australia liveable and maintains some genuinely good high-density housing. It also possesses extraordinary leverage when one company can simultaneously control a person's building, water, access systems, maintenance, services, and redevelopment rights.

The six oligarchies overlap heavily. They finance, power, secure, house, transport, insure, and medically support one another while also competing. Cross-holdings, public pension ownership, joint ventures, shared boards, and executives moving between corporate and public institutions are normal.

## Reproductive infrastructure

The Severance made reproductive infrastructure one of the world's largest strategic industries.

It includes male genetic archives, assisted-conception clinics, genetic replication and quality control, lineage and diversity verification, fertility medicine, chain-of-custody and anti-tampering security, privacy and medical-data law, insurance and public-health systems, corporate reproductive providers, cross-border recognition and registry interoperability, black markets, stolen samples, falsified provenance, organised crime, and parentage/donor-rights/inheritance disputes.

This infrastructure is socially ordinary and economically enormous, but campaigns are not required to focus on it.

MORROW is one major private provider in this sector. SPHN and CALYX are additional established actors. No one institution owns the entire Australian reproductive system.

## Male registries

There is no single global male database.

Each serious jurisdiction maintains or recognises a registry system. Registries interoperate through treaties, cryptographic identity standards, medical agreements, genetic-bank protocols, and cross-border recognition.

ARIO manages interoperability standards and dispute processes, not one master list.

Implementation varies. One jurisdiction may maximise privacy and autonomy; another may be paternalistic; another may outsource much of the system to corporations; another may be corrupt or authoritarian.

The setting-wide baseline remains one registered genetic contribution. Registry status is not the same as freedom status.

### Protected, Claimed, and Free

- **Protected:** genuinely protected inside a managed family, civic, corporate, or communal structure; protection may also become paternalistic or restrictive.
- **Claimed:** effectively controlled through coercion, criminal capture, contract, dependency, guardianship, manipulation, wealth, isolation, or institutional power.
- **Free:** retains genuine autonomy and enough competence, allies, mobility, resources, legal leverage, or force to resist control.

These are social descriptions rather than universal legal castes.

## Relationships and sexual culture

Male-centred polyamory is socially normal but never mandatory. There is no canonical harem size and no assumption that women are automatically attracted to men.

Female/female relationships and adult sexual expression are mainstream. Glamorous or provocative female presentation does not automatically imply weakness, availability, unseriousness, or professional incompetence.

Romance, sex, and harem structures remain optional Campaign Plan content.

## Companion robots

Male-presenting companion and sex robots are common consumer technology. They range from cheap mechanical products to sophisticated luxury conversational companions.

They remain recognisably artificial. Cybernetically augmented humans remain biological humans rather than manufactured robots.

## Technology

Siren Protocol's technology target is advanced but recognisable future cyberpunk.

Expected elements include real firearms, realistic ammunition and reload constraints, cover-centric gunfights, cybernetic augmentation, neural interfaces, local and wide-area networking, wired access, removable/external HUDs, hacking, drones, AI, advanced medicine, barriers and shields, advanced vehicles, biotechnology, orbital communications/stations, and limited lunar industrial/research facilities.

The central principle is: **Connectivity creates capability and vulnerability.**

Advanced AI does not invalidate HACKERs. Drones do not eliminate human operators. Cybernetics do not silently become magic.

## Campaign freedom

Siren Protocol has no default player character, no required PC gender, no mandatory harem, no mandatory romance, no mandatory sexual content, no mandatory party composition, no mandatory campaign duration, and no mandatory campaign premise.

Campaign Plans may focus on tactical combat, corporate work, gangs, investigation, hacking, relationships, social play, rare-male politics, women-led operations, ordinary life, or any combination compatible with the setting.

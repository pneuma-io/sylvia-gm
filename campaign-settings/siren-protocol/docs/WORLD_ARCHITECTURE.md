# Siren Protocol — World Architecture

**Status:** creator-approved setting-wide canon  
**Approved:** 2026-08-27  
**Present year:** 2225

## Purpose

This document locks the broad physical, political, orbital, registry, reproductive-industry, Australian, showcase-region, and core-cast placement architecture of Siren Protocol. Focused Australian political, corporate, district, local-power, and character material is expanded under `setting/` and `characters/`.

## 1. Present year

The Siren Protocol present is **2225**.

Trigger Discipline itself remains era-flexible and may support present-day or other compatible settings. The year 2225 is a Siren Protocol Campaign Setting decision, not a Trigger Discipline Ruleset invariant.

The Severance remains described as roughly two centuries before the present. Its exact date does not need to be fixed until chronology requires it.

## 2. Recognisable Earth, successor politics

Earth's continents, oceans, major geographic regions, and inherited cultural/geographic labels remain recognisable.

Political geography is not required to preserve the exact states and borders of the early twenty-first century. Over the intervening centuries, present-day countries may have survived in evolved constitutional forms, merged into larger federations or blocs, fragmented into successor states, lost or gained territory, devolved meaningful power to cities or regions, corporatised parts of their administration, ceded zones to autonomous/treaty/criminal/corporate control, or disappeared while leaving strong cultural or geographic identities behind.

Use recognisable geography without pretending Siren Protocol can predict two centuries of real political history one-for-one.

No world government exists.

## 3. Earthbound, not technologically Earth-locked

Siren Protocol is overwhelmingly an Earth setting, but 2225 humanity possesses ordinary space infrastructure.

Canonical examples include satellites and dense orbital communications infrastructure; orbital stations and service platforms; military, scientific, industrial, and commercial orbital assets; and limited lunar research, extraction, fabrication, or industrial facilities.

This does **not** make Siren Protocol an interplanetary civilisation setting.

There is no default Mars civilisation, no major off-world population comparable to Earth, and no convenient colony society that causes Earth's demographic and political premises to stop mattering. Off-world locations may support individual adventures or specialised institutions without becoming the setting's primary social stage.

## 4. Male registries are jurisdictional, not one global database

The demographic incentive to preserve male genetic diversity is nearly universal, but there is no single world authority and therefore no single authoritative global male registry.

Each serious jurisdiction maintains or recognises its own registration system.

Registries interoperate through combinations of treaties, identity standards, cryptographic verification, genetic-bank protocols, medical interoperability agreements, cross-border recognition rules, and restricted lineage and contribution records.

Implementation varies by jurisdiction. One jurisdiction may maximise male autonomy and privacy; another may be paternalistic; another may rely heavily on corporate service providers; another may be corrupt or coercive.

The setting-wide obligation remains one registered genetic contribution intended to preserve male genetic diversity. The administrative mechanism used to enforce or recognise that obligation may differ locally.

A global master database should not be assumed unless a future creator decision explicitly establishes one.

## 5. Reproductive infrastructure is a major civilisational industry

The numbers created by the Severance make reproductive infrastructure one of the largest and most strategically important industries on Earth.

This sector includes male genetic archives and long-term preservation; assisted-conception clinics; genetic replication and quality control; lineage and diversity verification; fertility medicine; anti-tampering and chain-of-custody security; privacy and medical-data law; cross-jurisdiction recognition systems; insurance and payment infrastructure; public reproductive-health services; corporate reproductive-health providers; black-market samples and falsified provenance; organised crime targeting archives, clinics, identities, or access; and inheritance, parentage, donor-rights, and lineage disputes.

This infrastructure is socially ordinary and economically enormous. It does not mean every campaign must focus on reproduction, any more than modern campaigns must focus on banking, energy, or telecommunications merely because those systems structure society.

## 6. Australia in 2225

Australia became a major 2225 power by exploiting two linked advantages: its immense interior landscapes and its solar potential.

Across the twenty-first and twenty-second centuries, desert-scale solar generation, energy storage, transmission infrastructure, and enormous data-centre / compute systems became central to Australian wealth and strategic influence. Interior Australia hosts vast solar fields, storage systems, transmission corridors, compute campuses, and service settlements. Coastal Australia remains the largest concentration of population, capital, ports, logistics, culture, finance, and network exchange.

Australia's rise did not depend on one miracle company or one instant revolution. It emerged from turning sunlight, land, technical infrastructure, labour, and compute capacity into long-term leverage.

### From Canberra to concessionary oligarchy

Australia did not experience a clean corporate coup in which government disappeared.

Power **slowly migrated away from the older federal-and-state system centred on Canberra** as public institutions became increasingly dependent on private owners and operators of energy, compute, networks, logistics, medicine, reproductive infrastructure, housing, finance, security, and transport.

The key mechanism is an **infrastructure ratchet**. A small concession becomes easier to extend because the private operator already owns compatible stations, maintenance, power, data, ticketing, property, or equipment. After repeated extensions across generations, rebuilding a public alternative becomes harder than renewing the concession.

The same pattern repeated across energy, AI/compute, telecommunications, housing, ports, logistics, medicine, reproductive services, finance, security, water, and other essential systems.

Government still exists and matters in 2225. Courts, legislation, elections, taxation, policing, welfare, regulation, diplomacy, public administration, and public services have not vanished.

But government is no longer the uncontested apex of Australian power.

The practical system is a **competitive corporate oligarchy layered over surviving government institutions**. Major corporate blocs can bargain with governments as near-peer powers because they control infrastructure on which daily life and public administration depend. These corporations compete with one another rather than forming one unified corporate state.

Canberra remains historically and institutionally important as a centre of constitutional government, courts, archives, diplomacy, inter-jurisdiction agreements, and surviving federal administration. It is no longer sufficient to say that Canberra simply 'runs Australia.'

### Six principal oligarchies

Australia's dominant corporate blocs are:

- **INLAND Systems Group (`INLAND`)** — desert solar, storage, transmission, hyperscale compute, and AI infrastructure.
- **Meridian Transit & Development (`MERIDIAN`)** — high-speed transport, freight, ports, stations, and transport-led development; it controls the principal Sydney-Brisbane-Melbourne high-speed trunk.
- **Morrow Genomics & Health (`MORROW`)** — hospitals, reproductive medicine, genetics, cybernetics, pharmaceuticals, and genetic infrastructure.
- **Lattice Civic Systems (`LATTICE`)** — networks, finance, payments, insurance, identity, credentials, and trust infrastructure.
- **Palisade Defence & Security (`PALISADE`)** — private security, defence systems, drones, intelligence, emergency response, and critical-infrastructure protection.
- **Bluehaven Urban Systems (`BLUEHAVEN`)** — water, housing, property, utilities, urban services, and coastal resilience.

These are overlapping conglomerates with primary industrial spines, not clean monopolies. Cross-holdings, debt, public pension ownership, shared infrastructure, joint ventures, and executive movement between corporate and public institutions are normal.

Each also provides real public value. Their danger comes from structural dependence and concentrated power rather than universal malice.

### Second-tier corporate layer

The recurring Australian/Sydney second-tier corporations are:

- ASTER — aerospace/aviation;
- IRONLACE — advanced materials;
- TIDEGLASS — maritime systems;
- CALYX — distributed reproductive/family healthcare;
- VECTORLINE — vehicles and road mobility;
- EMBERLINE — industrial robotics;
- NORTHLIGHT — media and entertainment;
- VERDIGRIS — remediation and environmental engineering.

These firms are large enough to matter politically and operationally but do not possess the near-sovereign leverage of the six principal oligarchies.

## 7. Showcase region: Sydney coastal megaregion

The setting's authored showcase is the **Sydney coastal region of Australia**.

It is a vast urbanised coastal megaregion grown from Greater Sydney through the Illawarra/Wollongong corridor, Central Coast, and Newcastle/Hunter region. Exact 2225 administrative borders and the final official regional name remain open.

The Sydney region became one of Australia's main concentrations of population, capital, culture, network exchange, corporate headquarters, advanced medicine, reproductive services, private security, logistics, finance, manufacturing, research, and high-value technical work.

Its power is inseparable from inland Australia. The coast consumes energy and computation generated across enormous interior infrastructure systems while acting as one of the principal places where that power is financed, traded, governed, litigated, secured, and converted into political influence.

The default Sydney political texture is **some government, primarily corporate oligarchic competition, plus smaller powers**.

Sydney is explicitly a **patchwork of both** public and private jurisdiction. Public territory may remain formally governed while corporate infrastructure determines practical access and dependency; elsewhere concessions, charters, leases, development compacts, property rights, or service contracts grant corporations explicit functional or territorial powers.

Government is not decorative. It can arrest people, run courts, collect taxes, operate services, regulate companies, win public support, and sometimes defeat corporate interests.

Corporations are nevertheless the dominant power centres because ownership of energy, compute, networks, housing, transport, medicine, reproductive services, security, and logistics creates practical dependency. No single corporation owns Sydney; competition among corporate oligarchies is part of what keeps the region unstable and creates room for public institutions, gangs, Aboriginal/community organisations, unions, cooperatives, investigators, operators, and player characters to matter.

### Macro geography

The nine macro-zones are:

1. Harbour Core;
2. Eastern & Northern Coastal Enclaves;
3. Parramatta Civic Spine;
4. Western Megacity;
5. Western Gate;
6. Botany–South Industrial Arc;
7. Illawarra Works;
8. Central Coast Ribbon;
9. Newcastle–Hunter Complex.

The Blue Mountains / Hawkesbury / Nepean edge is a strategic geographic/infrastructure layer rather than a tenth urban zone.

### Initial playable district layer

The first complete district pass contains **40 districts** across the nine macro-zones, recorded in the three Sydney district files.

This layer establishes common-use geographic/play identities, not clean corporate territories or necessarily exact government boundaries.

### Jurisdiction stack

Scene-level authority may differ across seven simultaneous layers:

1. constitutional sovereignty;
2. municipal/regional law;
3. land ownership;
4. infrastructure concession;
5. network/identity authority;
6. contracted security;
7. practical street control.

The stack is the preferred way to reason about Sydney's overlapping sovereignty.

### Local power layer

The showcase region includes established public bodies SCCA, PCS, SPHN, ARIO, and CCFB; labour/professional bodies ETWF, IFU, MFG, ACWA, and GCEU; community/advocacy powers Fairfield Compact, South Creek Assembly, Coastal Country Accord, Open Civic League, and Free Status Network; and criminal/grey/hacker powers Redline Syndicate, Saltline, Brick Saints, Glasshouse, Null Choir, Black Kites, and the Night Market.

These groups have independent motives. They are not assumed to be proxies for one of the six oligarchies.

### Role contrast

- Vesper is the live-fire riflewoman; Sable is the patient investigator/overwatch marksman.
- Riot breaches; Kora protects, holds, and extracts.
- Pixie physically infiltrates; Hex digitally intrudes.
- Velvet moves public attention; Siren works private HUMINT and leverage.
- Blaze operates vehicles under pressure; Nova engineers and coordinates technical systems.
- Solace preserves life and medical continuity.

### Character mechanical boundary

The authored foundations establish fiction, not complete Trigger Discipline builds. They do not set final statistics, `SPECIALTY` paths, weapon values, armour, cybernetic bonuses, drone action economy, vehicle-combat rules, social modifiers, or hacking permissions.

The locked visual references establish appearance only. Generated labels, quotes, affiliations, stats, and equipment names inside those images remain non-canonical unless separately authored.

## 9. Still open

This architecture deliberately leaves open:

- the exact date of the Severance;
- the Severance's true cause, which may remain permanently undefined;
- the final official 2225 name and exact administrative boundaries of the Sydney coastal megaregion;
- the exact Australian successor constitutional structure and final names/statuses of surviving federal/state governments;
- individual oligarchy CEOs, boards, dynasties, executive factions, exact cross-holdings, and ownership percentages;
- exact ownership links between second-tier corporations and the six oligarchies;
- precise street/block-level property, concession, security, and jurisdiction maps beneath the established district layer;
- major named powers outside the Australian/Sydney showcase;
- deeper core-cast biography beyond the authored foundations, final Trigger Discipline builds, exact equipment/augmentation statistics, and any romance/sexual/harem relationships not separately authored.

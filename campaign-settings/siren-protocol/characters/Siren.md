## Role identity

Siren is a **club proprietor, dealmaker, and human-intelligence broker**. She represents the social/HUMINT control identity through reading motives, building trust, running sources, negotiating private arrangements, recognising leverage, and creating environments where people reveal more than they intended.

She is not Velvet with less fame. Velvet moves public attention; Siren works in rooms where everybody present is trying not to become public.

## Personality

Siren is poised, patient, attentive, and rarely hurried. She has a talent for making a person feel like the most important participant in a conversation without actually surrendering control of it.

She prefers truthful bargains to clever lies because lies create maintenance work. She is comfortable with vice, secrecy, and morally complicated clients, but has firm limits around trafficking, coercive ownership, and deals where one party is not meaningfully free to consent.

Siren remembers debts in both directions. Somebody who did her a kindness ten years ago may discover that she remembers it more clearly than they do.

## Background

Siren owns and operates **Blue Hour**, a high-end Harbour Core club and private meeting venue built deliberately around jurisdictional ambiguity: public street access, private membership rooms, corporate tenants, hospitality licensing, LATTICE identity layers, and carefully limited security contracts.

Blue Hour became valuable because powerful people from institutions that distrust one another can meet there without pretending they are friends. Executives, politicians, union officials, celebrities, journalists, lawyers, investigators, freight brokers, and criminals all use overlapping parts of the venue's social ecosystem.

Siren's information business grew from hospitality rather than espionage training. She learned that most secrets arrive voluntarily if the room is comfortable, the staff is trusted, and nobody is visibly taking notes.

She is not a neutral saint. Information is one of the things she trades. Her reputation survives because she is extremely selective about when she breaks confidence and because clients know some forms of coercion will get them permanently barred rather than quietly tolerated.

## Affiliations

- **Primary:** independent owner/operator of Blue Hour.
- **Connections:** hospitality networks, Saltline brokers, selected Glasshouse contacts, lawyers, political staff, corporate fixers, journalists, Harbour Core venue operators.
- **Not owned by:** any of the six oligarchies. LATTICE and BLUEHAVEN infrastructure are important to the venue, but dependency is not affiliation.
- **Typical operating areas:** Harbour Core, Bondi Shelf, high-end private venues, port/transport social networks.

## Established relationships

- **Velvet:** friendly rival and occasional collaborator. Velvet controls public attention at scale; Siren specialises in the private conversations that happen after the cameras leave.
- **Sable:** recurring professional relationship. Siren supplies human intelligence and introductions; Sable verifies what survives contact with records, surveillance, and physical evidence.

No romantic or sexual relationship is implied by either tie.

## Equipment identity

Siren uses secure personal communications, privacy tools, venue-access systems, discreet recording/counter-recording equipment, source-management infrastructure, and concealed personal defence appropriate to high-end environments.

Her value is not a hidden mind-control implant. Social outcomes must still come from Trigger Discipline social/investigation mechanics, leverage, context, and actual human choices.

## Use in play

Siren works naturally as:

- a pre-made companion for HUMINT, negotiation, crime, or intrigue campaigns;
- the owner of neutral-ish ground where enemies can speak without immediately shooting;
- an information source who wants a favour rather than money;
- a broker connecting PCs to a politician, criminal, union official, executive, or protected client;
- a social antagonist who never needs to threaten the party because she knows what everybody else wants from them.

Her central tension is **neutrality versus complicity**: the value of Blue Hour comes from being usable by people who hate one another, but every line Siren refuses to draw becomes a line somebody else will eventually cross inside her house.

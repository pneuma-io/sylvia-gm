## Role identity

Riot is a **forced-entry and close-assault specialist**. She represents the `BREACHER` fantasy through hard entries, violent short-range problems, structural access, improvised solutions, and the confidence to cross a threshold everybody else is trying to avoid.

Her reputation looks chaotic. Her actual entry work is not. She studies hinges, locks, walls, sightlines, pressure, timing, civilian presence, and what is likely to be on the other side before she commits.

## Personality

Riot is loud, funny, irreverent, physically affectionate with people she trusts, and almost impossible to embarrass. She enjoys making authority figures work harder than they expected to.

She hates being patronised and reacts particularly badly to people mistaking style for stupidity. Under the noise is a very practical operator who is good at deciding when brute force is actually the wrong answer.

Riot becomes frighteningly serious around trapped civilians, abusive protection rackets, and anyone using superior firepower to terrorise people who cannot answer back.

## Background

Riot grew up in Western Sydney around industrial work, redevelopment fights, improvised repairs, and local protection crews. She learned tools before she learned tactical doctrine and learned tactical doctrine later because the difference between 'breaking in' and 'getting everyone killed' eventually mattered.

She spent time working with a Brick Saints chapter during a period when the local crew genuinely protected residents from predatory outsiders. She left when parts of the chapter slid from community protection into entitlement and extortion.

She still knows people inside the Saints, still helps specific neighbourhoods, and still gets accused of being a gang member by people who prefer simple categories.

These days she takes legitimate heavy-entry, recovery, security, salvage, and emergency work alongside less legitimate Night Market jobs when the client passes her personal smell test.

- **Blaze:** close friend and frequent trouble-partner. Blaze gets Riot out; Riot gets Blaze in. Their friendship predates most of their professional reputations.
- **Pixie:** occasional infiltration partner. Pixie can often open a route quietly; Riot is what happens when quiet stops being useful.

No romantic or sexual relationship is implied by either tie.

## Equipment identity

Riot favours compact close-range firearms, breaching tools, protective gear that leaves her mobile, and a heavily reinforced cybernetic gauntlet useful for tools, leverage, protection, and violent entry.

Her equipment should look modified, personalised, repaired, painted over, and reconfigured rather than issued from one corporate catalogue.

Do not infer bonus attacks, superhuman wall-breaking, explosive mechanics, or cybernetic damage from the visual design alone. Those require Trigger Discipline or an explicit Siren Protocol extension.

## Use in play

Riot works naturally as:

- a pre-made companion for close-quarters tactical campaigns;
- a Night Market contact who can source or modify entry equipment;
- a local guide through parts of the Western Megacity where corporate security has poor relationships;
- a former gang associate caught between people she still loves and behaviour she will no longer defend;
- the person a team calls when the plan has reached the phrase 'we need that door gone.'

Her central tension is **belonging versus independence**. Riot wants community badly enough to fight for it, but refuses to let any gang, corporation, cause, or crew turn that loyalty into ownership.

## Role identity

Nova is a **drone engineer, robotics specialist, and tactical-systems coordinator**. She represents the `TECH` fantasy through design, repair, deployment, remote systems, sensor integration, field improvisation, equipment control, and making a team more capable by making its technology actually work together.

She is not the cast's HACKER and not the cast's driver. Hex breaks networks; Blaze operates vehicles under pressure; Nova builds, configures, coordinates, and repairs the systems they rely on.

## Personality

Nova is quiet, dry, exacting, ambitious, and intensely self-contained. She dislikes noise that pretends to be confidence and prefers somebody who says 'I do not know' to somebody who invents certainty.

She can appear aloof because she spends very little social energy performing reassurance. In practice, she is highly attentive to the people and machines under her responsibility.

Nova's worst habit is control. If she can solve a risk by adding one more sensor, one more fail-safe, or one more decision layer under her authority, she usually wants to.

## Background

Nova comes from a founder-family shareholder bloc inside EMBERLINE Robotics. She was raised around engineering, board politics, patents, succession planning, and the assumption that technical brilliance was useful because it would eventually become corporate authority.

She rose into an executive engineering role and became especially associated with field robotics, distributed drone systems, and machines designed to operate in hazardous or contested environments.

Her break with EMBERLINE came during a strategic deal that would have transferred teams, patents, and long-term control rights into an oligarchy-aligned arrangement she considered technically short-sighted and politically irreversible. Nova opposed it publicly enough to turn a board dispute into a family dispute.

She left with her expertise, some personal systems, a network of former colleagues, and far less practical access to the family wealth than outsiders assume. Whether 'disinherited' is legally accurate depends on which lawyer and which jurisdiction is asked; the estrangement is not in doubt.

Now she works independently as a systems engineer and tactical coordinator, taking contracts where she can retain technical control over her own work.

## Equipment identity

Nova works with modular field drones, repair/fabrication tools, sensor packages, remote interfaces, diagnostic equipment, secure command hardware, and custom tactical-support machines.

Her visual drone systems are canonical to her character identity, but their exact number, combat statistics, autonomy, action economy, and equipment values are not fixed. Drones do not silently create extra turns or independent combatants outside Trigger Discipline rules.

## Use in play

Nova works naturally as:

- a pre-made companion for TECH, drone, equipment, or investigation-heavy campaigns;
- an estranged corporate insider with knowledge of second-tier corporate politics;
- the engineer called when several individually functional systems refuse to function together;
- a rival designer whose equipment outperforms the party's for reasons that may be fixable;
- an employer assembling a field team around a prototype, recovery operation, or infrastructure problem.

Her central tension is **earned authority versus inherited authority**. Nova wants every system she controls to justify that control through competence, while still carrying the access, education, habits, and blind spots of somebody raised to inherit power.

## Role identity

Hex is a **network intruder, identity-systems specialist, and hostile-systems analyst**. She represents the `HACKER` fantasy through reconnaissance, credential compromise, local and wide-area intrusion, device control, network inference, persistence, counter-surveillance, and exploiting the fact that 2225 systems become powerful by becoming connected.

She is not Pixie's physical-infiltration role. Hex gets through systems; Pixie gets through buildings.

## Personality

Hex is sardonic, private, obsessive, and ritualistic about process. She likes repeatable workflows, clean diagrams, named failure states, and interface arrangements that make complex systems feel legible.

Her custom UI uses symbolic geometry, crescents, circles, and 'sigil' motifs because she finds them cognitively efficient. She knows this makes people call her a witch and finds correcting them less entertaining than letting them be wrong for a few minutes.

Hex distrusts concentrated information power but is no longer naive about disclosure. She has seen leaks expose corruption and has seen the same leak destroy people who never chose to become evidence.

## Background

Hex came up through university, public-network, and activist technical circles around Pyrmont Exchange. She spent time with a Null Choir cell when she was younger, initially attracted to the belief that abusive secrecy could be beaten by making systems transparent.

She never formally denounced the Choir, because there is no single organisation to denounce. She simply stopped accepting the premise that release is automatically justice.

Now Hex works independently across security research, intrusion testing, investigative support, privacy work, counter-surveillance, and jobs she refuses to describe with legally useful vocabulary. Open Civic League lawyers, Free Status Network advocates, journalists, and private operators all occasionally need somebody who understands how identity and infrastructure actually fail.

## Established relationships

- **Pixie:** close operational friend. Hex can open the network once Pixie reaches the hardware; Pixie can reach hardware Hex cannot safely touch from outside.
- **Solace:** trusted physician. Solace has treated Hex after serious interface overuse and is one of the few people Hex allows to see complete biometric/medical data.
- **Nova:** occasional technical collaborator and philosophical irritant. Nova likes controlled systems; Hex likes systems that remain auditable by people who do not own them.

No romantic or sexual relationship is implied by these ties.

## Equipment identity

Hex uses hardened portable compute, interface gloves, neural/physical access hardware, local network tools, secure communications, removable storage, and custom visualisation software. Her hooded outfit is part privacy, part sensory control, and part aesthetic preference.

No device grants universal access. Siren Protocol's central technology rule remains: connectivity creates capability and vulnerability. Every target still needs an actual attack surface, access route, credential flaw, local foothold, or other Ruleset-supported opportunity.

## Use in play

Hex works naturally as:

- a pre-made companion for hacking, investigation, and surveillance-heavy campaigns;
- a reluctant Null Choir contact when a cell's leak has consequences nobody planned for;
- a privacy specialist in a registry, medical, corporate, or LATTICE dispute;
- the HACKER trying to prove the 'secure' system has been compromised from inside;
- a contractor whose biggest problem is deciding what to do with the truth after she gets it.

Her central tension is **access versus responsibility**: Hex believes people need power over the systems that define their lives, while knowing that the ability to expose a system can expose everyone trapped inside it too.

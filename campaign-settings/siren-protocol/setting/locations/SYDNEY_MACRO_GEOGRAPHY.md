# Sydney Coastal Region — Macro Geography

**Status:** creator-approved showcase-region canon  
**Present year:** 2225  
**Scope:** Sydney coastal megaregion, Australia  
**Naming note:** the macro-zone names below are development / geographic descriptors unless separately promoted as formal 2225 administrative names.

## Purpose

The Sydney coastal showcase region is a **continuous urban megaregion with multiple centres of gravity**, not one uniform city and not a set of clean corporate territories.

For campaign and setting design, the megaregion is divided into nine major macro-zones plus a western geographic edge. These zones are broad planning areas used to organise later districts, factions, jurisdictions, ordinary-life material, and introductory scenarios.

Recognisable inherited names such as Sydney, Parramatta, Wollongong, Newcastle, the Central Coast, the Blue Mountains, Hawkesbury, and Nepean remain useful everyday geographic references even if formal 2225 jurisdictions later use different names.

The political map overlays the physical map. A macro-zone can simultaneously contain public government, explicit corporate jurisdiction, private infrastructure, community-controlled areas, union power, organised crime, and ordinary private property.

## 1. Harbour Core

**Geographic basis:** Sydney CBD, Pyrmont, Barangaroo, North Sydney, and the inner harbour.

The Harbour Core is the globally recognisable face of the megaregion: finance, corporate headquarters, executive services, elite housing, public institutions, high-end medicine, international business, and dense network exchange.

LATTICE is especially influential through finance, identity, payments, communications, and commercial infrastructure. BLUEHAVEN owns or manages major vertical property portfolios. MORROW maintains premium medical and specialist facilities. MERIDIAN controls major underground and interchange infrastructure. PALISADE is common inside corporate towers and high-security properties.

Public government is highly visible here, but visibility does not imply exclusive control. A pedestrian can move from a public plaza into a privately governed tower, a corporate concourse, a MERIDIAN transport zone, or a high-security campus without crossing a traditional political border.

The Harbour Core should look spectacular, dense, expensive, and intensely managed rather than uniformly grim.

## 2. Eastern and Northern Coastal Enclaves

**Geographic basis:** eastern suburbs, lower north shore, and high-value harbour/beach districts.

These are some of the most expensive and desirable residential areas in Australia, preserved and expanded through generations of coastal engineering, water infrastructure, managed development, and private security.

BLUEHAVEN, MORROW, wealthy families, investment vehicles, and private interests are particularly strong. PALISADE is common in high-value compounds and managed precincts.

The setting should resist the idea that wealth must look dystopian. These areas can be genuinely beautiful: clean beaches, excellent transit, resilient infrastructure, lush managed landscapes, advanced medicine, safe public spaces, and extraordinary housing.

Their cyberpunk edge comes from exclusion, ownership, access control, private jurisdiction, redevelopment pressure, and the amount of infrastructure required to keep this quality of life functioning.

## 3. Parramatta Civic Spine

**Geographic basis:** Parramatta, Olympic Park, and adjoining inner-western civic/education centres.

The Parramatta Civic Spine is one of the strongest concentrations of surviving **public institutional power** in the showcase region.

It contains major courts, administrative bodies, universities, public hospitals, regional agencies, civic archives, public-interest institutions, and government-owned or strongly regulated infrastructure.

Corporations remain deeply embedded, but public authorities here are more capable than in many other parts of the megaregion of refusing a corporate demand and making that refusal stick.

This makes Parramatta politically important rather than merely a secondary business district. Oligarchies lobby, litigate, negotiate, fund, pressure, and recruit here because public law still has teeth.

## 4. Western Megacity

**Geographic basis:** the huge western residential and employment belt around Blacktown, Penrith, Liverpool, and adjoining urban centres.

This is the population engine of the Sydney coastal region: dense mixed-income housing, schools, hospitals, fabrication, warehouses, retail, entertainment, local businesses, workshops, logistics, public services, and enormous residential developments.

BLUEHAVEN is heavily present through housing and urban utilities, while LATTICE, MERIDIAN, MORROW, and smaller corporations are embedded throughout daily life.

The Western Megacity should **not** default to a cyberpunk slum. It contains wealth, poverty, stable middle-income life, good neighbourhoods, neglected areas, public housing, corporate housing, independent businesses, immigrant and cultural communities, organised labour, gangs, cooperatives, and local political machines.

This is where a huge share of ordinary Sydney actually lives.

## 5. Western Gate

**Geographic basis:** Badgerys Creek / western aerotropolis and the major freight, airport, and transport interchange zone around it.

Western Gate is one of MERIDIAN's strongest areas of influence.

It contains airport infrastructure, high-speed rail connections, autonomous freight systems, logistics campuses, warehousing, interchanges, corporate development, maintenance facilities, security zones, and access to the main inland/east-coast transport networks.

PALISADE is heavily present because of critical infrastructure and transport security. LATTICE is deeply embedded in ticketing, identity, cargo, and payment systems. INLAND infrastructure feeds major power and compute requirements.

Western Gate is an ideal demonstration of the concessionary state: much of the surrounding land can remain under public sovereignty while the routes, stations, systems, and commercially useful spaces through it are privately controlled.

## 6. Botany–South Industrial Arc

**Geographic basis:** Mascot, Port Botany, Kurnell, and the southern industrial/coastal infrastructure belt.

The Botany–South Industrial Arc contains the systems glamorous Sydney prefers not to think about: ports, warehousing, heavy logistics, fabrication, desalination, utilities, chemical and materials handling, freight exchange, industrial maintenance, and infrastructure servicing.

MERIDIAN, BLUEHAVEN, PALISADE, and INLAND overlap heavily here. Organised labour and organised crime also matter because control of a chokepoint, workforce, port gate, maintenance contractor, or freight schedule can create enormous leverage.

This zone should support industrial tactical play without becoming a generic wasteland. It is busy, productive, heavily networked, highly automated, and economically essential.

## 7. Illawarra Works

**Geographic basis:** Wollongong, Port Kembla, and the southern coastal industrial corridor.

The Illawarra Works is a major centre of heavy fabrication, engineering, advanced materials, maritime industry, large-scale repair, industrial research, and parts of Australia's orbital/lunar supply chain.

The region retains a particularly strong tradition of **organised labour, professional associations, and cooperative power**. Major corporations possess concessions and facilities here, but unions and worker organisations can still halt production or maintenance at a scale large enough to force serious negotiation.

This makes the Illawarra one of the best places in the setting to show non-government, non-corporate institutional power that is neither criminal nor merely symbolic.

MERIDIAN and INLAND are important, PALISADE protects major sites, and specialist second-tier industrial firms should eventually be numerous.

## 8. Central Coast Ribbon

**Geographic basis:** Gosford, Wyong, and the continuous urban/coastal corridor between Greater Sydney and Newcastle.

The Central Coast Ribbon is the connective tissue of the megaregion: commuter centres, housing, tourism, logistics, network campuses, station development, older coastal communities, managed new towns, and infrastructure corridors.

It is deliberately one of the most **jurisdictionally fragmented** parts of the showcase region.

Municipal government, MERIDIAN station property, BLUEHAVEN housing estates, public roads, community institutions, syndicates, private developments, and semi-autonomous local organisations can exist within a few kilometres of one another.

It should feel less vertically monumental than the Harbour Core and more like two centuries of suburban, coastal, transit-oriented, and corporate growth layered on top of one another.

## 9. Newcastle–Hunter Complex

**Geographic basis:** Newcastle, its port, and the Hunter industrial region.

Newcastle–Hunter is a northern counterweight to central Sydney rather than simply a distant suburb.

It contains a major port, freight and fabrication systems, industrial research, advanced manufacturing, energy interfaces, heavy logistics, and orbital-industry supply chains.

INLAND and MERIDIAN are powerful here, with PALISADE and specialist industrial firms heavily involved. Municipal institutions, labour organisations, universities, local corporate interests, and regional political bodies retain their own power bases.

Newcastle should have its **own political and corporate culture**. A decision made in the Harbour Core does not automatically settle a dispute in Newcastle.

This prevents the showcase region from having only one centre of power.

## 10. Blue Mountains / Hawkesbury / Nepean Edge

This is a geographic and infrastructure layer rather than a tenth urban macro-zone.

The Blue Mountains, Hawkesbury, Nepean, reservoirs, older settlements, protected landscapes, transmission corridors, transport chokepoints, water infrastructure, and interfaces with inland energy/compute systems form the western edge of the dense coastal megaregion.

This area gives Siren Protocol access to wilderness, semi-rural settlements, reservoirs, infrastructure corridors, remote facilities, older communities, and terrain that is not swallowed by tower-city urbanism.

It is also strategically important because water, transport, transmission, data links, and inland infrastructure must pass through or around constrained geography to reach the coast.

INLAND, BLUEHAVEN, MERIDIAN, public authorities, Aboriginal organisations and land interests, conservation bodies, communities, and infrastructure operators can all have legitimate competing interests here.

## Overlapping authority is the map

These macro-zones are **not faction territories**.

A single block in the Harbour Core could be:

- public land at street level;
- a BLUEHAVEN-managed tower above it;
- LATTICE-controlled identity and payment infrastructure throughout;
- a MORROW clinic on several floors;
- a MERIDIAN concourse underground;
- PALISADE security inside privately controlled spaces;
- powered by INLAND infrastructure;
- subject to public criminal law and a separate set of contractual access rules.

The district map therefore needs multiple layers rather than one colour per controlling faction.

Later district design should track at least:

- formal public jurisdiction;
- corporate property and concession boundaries;
- infrastructure ownership;
- security/enforcement provider;
- effective street-level control;
- network/identity provider;
- community, union, gang, syndicate, or other local power;
- major transport and utility dependencies.

## Visual diversity

The Sydney coastal megaregion must not look uniformly like a neon cyberpunk downtown.

- The Harbour Core can be intensely vertical and technologically saturated.
- Western Sydney can look like enormous dense contemporary urbanism evolved forward.
- The Illawarra and Botany arcs can be active industrial landscapes.
- The Central Coast can preserve beaches, older communities, suburban forms, and new transit-oriented centres.
- wealthy coastal enclaves can look almost utopian;
- public civic precincts can be clean, institutional, and recognisably governmental;
- neglected pockets can show infrastructure decay without defining the whole city;
- the Blue Mountains/Hawkesbury/Nepean edge can remain green, rugged, infrastructural, and comparatively low-density.

Contrast is part of the setting's identity.

## Deliberately open

This macro-geography does **not** yet lock:

- the formal 2225 name of the overall megaregion;
- formal administrative names or borders for the nine macro-zones;
- exact district and neighbourhood names beneath each macro-zone;
- district-by-district corporate influence;
- gang, syndicate, union, community, or political faction names;
- exact locations of major reproductive campuses, genetic archives, defence sites, orbital interfaces, or corporate headquarters;
- which of the eleven core characters live, work, or operate in any zone.
